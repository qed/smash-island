# Boss design: NINE (Algebralien Boss Rush, boss 3 of 9)

Written 2026-11-02 for the "pipis" Algebralien Boss Rush. Read-only study of `ii-dlc`; nothing in the game was edited.
All frame counts are 60 fps. All pixel numbers are logical pixels in the game's 1100 x 720 view (the camera size), so they stay true on a bigger stage.
Pronouns: the wiki's own article uses they/them for Nine (the wiki also says Nine is the only character who uses every pronoun), so this file does too.

## 0. The pitch in five lines

- Nine is the rush's skater: the only boss that is on the ground, fast, and made of stunts. Two and Four float and pop; Nine rolls.
- THE SIGNATURE: Nine's feet are glued to the board, so every stunt is a commitment. The lane and the landing are fixed when the wind-up ends, and Nine cannot stop, turn or cancel. Dodge the stunt and Nine comes out of it exposed (wipeout, or a stuck landing) and takes +50% damage until back up.
- Five attacks: TUBULAR! (the board run; from act 2 also the motorcycle run), CLEANUP CREW!, BOOYAH!, STONE-TO-BOOT! (act 2), GRIND!.
- Two acts: "Totally Tubular" (radical, learn the stunts) and "Not Radical" (the planet cracks, the bike and the boot join, Nine gets angry).
- Defeat: Nine ends up stuck on a drop tower with lava coming (a real gag from the 2,763,000 and 3,517,000 subscriber specials).

### Who Nine is
- An Algebralien, the Arabic numeral 9, an Integer. First seen in The Number Playground Chronicle! (non-canon); first scripted in the 800,000 special; first BFDI-universe appearance in Seasonal Shift; last in All For One. Voiced by Sam Thornbury. Placed 2nd (tied with Seven) in Battle for Permission to Meet 10. Two deaths on the wiki, zero kills.
- Look: white-ish body (Mystic #dde2ec), lighter white limbs, black outline, black sunglasses whose shine uses the skateboard's three colours: Sunglow #fed629, Cream #ffffce, Malibu #6ea9fd. The sunglasses and board come and go across episodes (lost in the 1,500,000 special; a bag on the head in the 2,000,000 special; round glasses in the 2,763,000 special).
- Personality: an exaggerated 1990s-2000s skater: slang, sunglasses, always cool, in their own words the coolest. Cheerful and soft underneath: cries easily (Six says they have no style, they glue their feet to the board), cares for the Skateboarding Aliens, gets upset when the BFDI Mouth takes the title of coolest. Not a fighter: the relationships page says Nine is not a top defender when things turn dangerous. The wiki also notes Nine may have glue for skin (they say it is what happens when you stand still for months).
- Powers (wiki): the Power of Nine from the Yoylite meteor (a white aura in Homeward Bound!); gravity manipulation (rides a motorcycle across Evil Canyon without falling, 1,500,000 special); explosion and shrapnel resistance (the same motorcycle explodes against a tree, Nine is unhurt). One absorbed Nine's power in Alone; in Homeward Bound! the wiki shows Nine drained (no sunglasses).
- Catchphrase (the one quote in this file): "Not radical." (That's What Foes Are For). The slang list also includes "tubular", "booyah", "bro", "fam", "dawgs".

## 2. PLACE IN THE RUSH: position 3 of 9, 1,400 HP

- HP: **1,400** (the owner's curve: Two 1,000, Four 1,200, Nine 1,400, Six 1,600 ...). I read 1,400 as the number on the bar for one fighter, so the usual x1.5 (BOSS_HP_MULT) and the +60%-per-extra-fighter rule must not be stacked on it by accident: the lead decides that for all nine.
- Phase thresholds in the engine are fixed at 66% and 33% (`updateBossAttack`), so 1,400 HP gives act 1 = 1,400 to 924, act 2 = 924 to 462, tempo bump = 462 to 0.
- Difficulty feel: third of nine, a step above Two and Four in HP but easier to read than either: Nine is on the floor and gives you a window after every dodge. What the old bot harness can say (`scripts/boss-rush-balance.json`, 15 bot fighters, 16 seeds, a 90 s cap, normal-rush HP): the grounded, hittable Springy (330 HP) was beaten in 79% of runs, Two (285) in 43%, Four (340) in 36%. So at the same HP a hittable ground boss is much easier to damage than a flier, which is why Nine's threat has to come from speed, reach and the cost of mistakes, not from a tough body. That harness cannot run Nine until it is built, and its 90 s cap is too short for any 1,000+ HP rush boss (a bot does about 7.5 HP/s, so 675 HP in 90 s): measure Nine with a 240 s cap.
- Fight length (estimate from that same file: seven bosses averaged about 7.5 HP of damage per second for the bots, 6.5 to 9): 1,400 / 7.5 = about 187 s, three minutes for a bot-grade fighter. A good player should do better; I have no human data and will not invent any. Act by act for a bot: about 1 min, 1 min, 1 min. The +50% windows should shave about a tenth off. For context only: the owner's whole curve sums to 17,200 HP, about 38 minutes of fighting at the bot rate, before deaths. If that is too long the lever is a rush-wide damage scale on fighter hits against bosses, not Nine's design.
- Stocks: the owner says start at 4, +2 after each boss, max 6. A player who lost nothing is already at the cap of 6 after Two, so by Nine the reward only matters if stocks were lost; Nine is played with 4-6 stocks and 3 minutes of fight ahead, which is why his windows matter. The old engine rule (+1 stock every third boss, `BOSS_STOCK_EVERY` = 3) must be replaced for the event; not Nine-specific.
- The rush's stages are larger than the normal arenas. I do not know the final size. Everything about Nine is therefore written relative to THE TARGET (the fighter the wind-up locked on) and to the camera's 1100 x 720, never to the stage's edges. See "Big-stage contract" below.

### Big-stage contract (what makes Nine work on any size of stage)
1. Nine cruises at about 380 px from the target. If the target is farther than 700 px, Nine pushes hard (14 px/frame) until close again: a skater is always faster than a runner (a fighter's top run is 6.4 px/frame), so nobody outruns Nine.
2. Every move starts near the target and is measured from the target's position at the lock, not from a wall. Nothing starts off the stage; a few things (crew boards, the motorcycle) start just off the camera's edge on Nine's side.
3. Runs do not have a fixed length. They end when they have overtaken the locked fighter by 260 px (cap 1,700 px of travel, or the stage's end margin). That is what stops the "just run away" answer: running away only makes the run longer, and the exposure window always ends up within reach of where the fighter is.
4. No pass is ever faster than 24 px/frame (Firey Speaker Box's board passes are 18-22). On a larger stage the fix is more distance, never more speed.
5. GRIND! needs a platform at least 360 px long (the picker drops it from the list otherwise). The stage's platforms are the rails: the decor draws a rail on every platform and a quarter-pipe at each end of one at least 360 px long.
6. Kick directions (the boot) point toward the stage's middle, so the move never throws a fighter off the world.

## 3. ARENA: THE SKATE PARK (new, simple; key `skatepark`)

Canon basis: Alone opens in Nine's unnamed skatepark on the skateboard planet (a loop, a slope, a cliff edge), and One destroys that planet at the end. The game already has art for the planet in five pieces (`attacks/oneskate.png`, File:Nine's Planet.png), made for One's OUT OF ORBIT!; the decor can reuse it. I considered reusing the Announcer's Cake at Stake place (Nine does host Cake at Skate in Seasonal Shift) and recommend against it: that arena belongs to a build-specific set (podiums via annDress) and Nine in a skate park is the stronger set piece.
- Sky: dusk, deep indigo to hot pink ("#1b1450" to "#e0609a"), stars, a ringed planet far back, parallax in screen space so a scrolling camera keeps it.
- Ground: concrete with a painted lane (yellow, cream, blue stripes from the board's colours), dust takes the concrete grey.
- Decor (all code, about 120 lines, per camera view so a long stage just repeats it): quarter-pipes every 1,100 px and at the stage ends; string lights across the back; a collapsed skate loop in the far background (canon: the aliens' loop collapses); a rail drawn along the top of every platform; on every platform at least 360 px long, a quarter-pipe at each end (decor only: the players never touch it, Nine's path uses it).
- Phase 2: the planet behind cracks into five pieces and drifts (swap to the `oneskate` art), the string lights go out one by one across 6 seconds, the pink in the sky drains to violet. Decor only.
- Hazard: none. The stage's own edges are the hazard, and the boot kicks inward on purpose.

## 5. THE SIGNATURE MECHANIC: GLUED TO THE BOARD (commit, dodge, punish)

- Nine cannot stop, turn or cancel a stunt (canon: the feet are superglued to the board, so they cannot even kick). Every stunt's lane, direction and landing spot lock 18-20 frames before it starts.
- Every stunt that can be dodged ends with Nine EXPOSED: a WIPEOUT after the long runs (the board snags, Nine tumbles over it and lies flat, canon tumbles and crashes), a STUCK LANDING after the jump and the grind (a short crouch, a pose). While exposed Nine cannot attack, and takes +50% damage. The gap to the next turn starts when Nine is back up.
- Window lengths (frames): board run 56 / 50 / 44 by tier, bike 44 / 38 after the explosion, grind 30 / 26 / 22, BOOYAH! 26 / 22 / 20 (only after the last landing of a pair). CLEANUP CREW! and STONE-TO-BOOT! leave no window (their reward is that they are slow and long).
- The window is shown by the body and nothing else: the tumble, the flat NineScared pose, a pulse of cream glow on the body (no icon, no bar, no line).
- Why it is different from the rest of the rush (as far as I can see; I cannot see the other seven designs): Two's rule is a grounding shield, Four's is the exit room; every other boss is untouchable between its attacks and there is no rule that dodging an attack OPENS the boss. Check Six, Zero, X, One, Three and 2,763 for another "punish window" before this is final.
- The +50% needs one line outside the slots, in `damageSummon` next to Two's `twoUngroundedBlocks` call (the owner's slot rule has this precedent). Option: drop the multiplier and keep only the window; the window alone is enough to be readable, the multiplier is what makes the window worth the walk.

## 6. THE FIVE ATTACKS

Damage is in boss hits: one boss hit = `bossDmg()` = 22% (times the loop multiplier); the existing shots are 0.8 of that. One attack id per move, so a fighter takes at most one hit from a move (the project's "same damage, harder" rule). Every wind-up reads from the body (a pose, a glow colour, a beat count in `BOSS_TELLS`, then the last 14 frames gather and flicker); no lines, rings or banners; the only world tells are the real kind the owner allows (a real shadow under what falls, dust and debris, a glow at a source). Anything that appears grows in over 12 frames. Falling bodies hit while falling. Wind-ups never shrink with the tier; speed and the second parts grow instead. Nine gets no `BOSS_TEL_PACE` entry (pace 1.0): a fast boss, with the 1.2 gap pace the roster rows share.

The ledge rule (the owner, 2026-10-04: a fighter on a platform must not be nearly safe): in act 2 four of the five moves reach a platform (the crew's platform lane, BOOYAH!'s landing, the boot's landing and GRIND!); the board and bike runs are floor moves by design. Boss and boot landings come down on the surface under the fighter (the platform's top or the floor), as Two's sun does.

### 1. TUBULAR! (board run; canon: the ride, "tubular", the glued feet)
- Tell (44 f): Nine backrolls 170 px away from you for the first 28 frames (they are winding up: the pose `coil`, drawn back away from you), the glow is the board's Malibu blue (#6ea9fd), 2 beats; the last 14 frames Nine crouches low (the gather) and the wheels throw sparks and dust. The target locks 18 frames before the push.
- Run: eases in over 12 frames then rides at 16 / 18 / 20 px/frame (act 1 / 2 / 3) straight at the locked fighter's lock spot, on the floor, and keeps going until it has overtaken that fighter by 260 px (cap 1,700 px, or the stage's end margin). The hit is a circle of radius 46 centred 46 px above the floor (a jump clears it with about 22 frames to spare; the same size as Firey Speaker Box's pass circle, 44). Damage 1.0, knockback mostly upward (vx 6 along the run, vy -14): a bump, never a launch into the void. A trail ribbon in the board's three colours fades behind it over 24 frames (visual only).
- End: the board snags; Nine tumbles twice over it in 24 frames, lies flat, gets up in 14 frames. Exposed 56 / 50 / 44 frames in all.
- Difference from Firey Speaker Box's ROCKET BOARD! (a locked row, edge to edge, no ending): this one starts beside you, can end anywhere, and its ending is the opening.

### 1b. TUBULAR!, motorcycle form (act 2; canon: Evil Canyon motorcycle, gravity manipulation, explosion resistance)
- Counts as the signature's second form, as Four's and Firey Speaker Box's signatures have two (`kind` in BOSS_PICK); the board form stays in the list. Never the same form twice in a row (the picker's variety rule already does it).
- Tell (52 f): a headlight glow and an engine roar build at the camera's edge on Nine's side (a glow at the source), Nine hops off the board and swings a leg over the bike that drives in from there; at frame 0 a dead tree grows in over 12 frames 760 px past the locked fighter's spot (a landmark, not a line). Pose `crouch`, glow orange (#ff8a2a), 2 beats.
- Run: 22 px/frame (24 in act 3), circle radius 58 centred 58 px above the floor (a jump clears it in about 13 frames: narrower than the board's, hence the longer wind-up). Damage 1.1.
- Blast: at the tree the bike explodes: a blast of radius 100 for 6 frames (damage 1.0, up and out), and eight shards fan back toward where the bike came from, speeds 7-12, gravity, each radius 14, damage 0.6, landing 150-520 px out. Nine walks out of the fire unharmed (canon resistance). Exposed 44 / 38 frames, the shards still falling during the first of them: the window sits at the tree, in the clear, and the cost of taking it is standing near the edge of the fire.
- Cut candidate if the build is too big: the whole of 1b (about 110 lines, a drawn bike and a tree). Without it Nine has four attacks in act 1 and five in act 2 (the boot joins); with it the signature has two forms. Keep it if you can: it is the single coolest thing in the fight.

### 2. CLEANUP CREW! (canon: the Skateboarding Aliens as Nine's cleanup crew, skating the park)
- Tell (40 f): Nine raises an arm and points to the side the boards will come from (pose `rear`, mirrored so the arm shows the side), dark blue glow (#2e4a9e, the aliens' colour), 3 beats (a three-count), dust from the far edge of the camera.
- Boards: they enter 640 px from the target (just off the camera) and roll across the floor at 8 / 9 / 10 px/frame, 1,500 px each, 4 / 5 / 6 of them with uneven gaps (150, 150, 270 px, repeat) so a jump has a rhythm to read. Each is the aliens' art (dark blue board, a pair of eyes) drawn about 50 px tall; hit circle radius 28, sitting on the floor (a hop clears it). Damage 0.6, knockback 9. One attack id for the whole crew.
- Act 2: three more boards roll along the top of the nearest platform the other way, a second after the floor boards start (a rail lane: the ledge rule). Act 3: the floor boards turn at the end of their travel (up a quarter-pipe, an arc), wait 40 frames and come back 2 px/frame faster (canon: the aliens skate a loop).

### 3. BOOYAH! (canon: the entrance flip, the word, the skater's trick)
- Tell (38 f): one deep crouch (pose `crouch`, one beat, cream glow #ffffce) then up; the aim follows you and locks 14 frames before takeoff. A shadow grows in on the surface under the fighter over 12 frames from takeoff and grows as Nine comes down (a real shadow under what falls).
- Flight: 54 f (act 1) / 48 / 44, apex 240 px above the landing surface. From frame 14 to 36 Nine does a flip: a hit circle of radius 100 around the body, damage 0.8, knockback 12 (the catch for a jumper; the tag `air`). The falling body hits while it falls (radius 76, damage 1.0, vx 10 vy -12). The landing throws two shock waves along the floor, one each way, radius 26, 9 px/frame, 420 px each, damage 0.7 (a hop clears them; not thrown when Nine lands on a platform: a dust ring instead).
- Act 2: a pair: a second ollie 20 frames after the first landing, to where the fighter moved. The stuck-landing window (26 / 22 / 20 f) comes after the last landing only. Phase 3 is the same with a faster flight.

### 4. STONE-TO-BOOT! (act 2; canon: the stone boot made to kick Eight out of Eight's spot)
- Tell (46 f): Nine cranks a machine (pose `shake`, stone-grey glow #8d8f98, 3 beats), pebbles and dust shake loose over the target's spot and a real boot-shaped shadow grows in over 12 frames and follows the fighter until 14 frames before the fall.
- The boot (code-drawn: grey stone, cracks, strata) falls 14 frames from 420 px up: hit while falling radius 72, damage 1.1, knockback up and a little inward. On landing: `impact()` (shake, dust, debris, floor scar). 12 frames later it KICKS along the floor toward the stage's middle: 240 px in 12 frames, radius 70, damage 0.9, knockback vx 20 inward vy -10 (kicked out of your spot, never off the stage). It rests 30 frames, then sinks and fades over 12.
- Act 3: a second boot, 32 frames after the first kick, on the fighter's new spot, with the same tell, shadow first.

### 5. GRIND! (a skater's trick; not canon, a game invention in the spirit of the skatepark)
- Tell (44 f): Nine pumps toward the quarter-pipe at the nearest platform end (rolls back 120 px, forward 160, two beats; pose `lunge`, Sunglow yellow glow #fed629), the platform's rail lights faintly cream (the rail is decor, so it is a glow at a source, not a lane line).
- Ride: up the ramp, then along the platform top at 13 / 15 / 17 px/frame, 40 px above its surface (sparks, a white flicker), hit circle radius 50 on the board's front: damage 0.9, knockback up 14 and 5 along the run. At the far end Nine ollies off: a 46-frame arc to the fighter's spot, which locks 20 frames before the leave. The falling body hits (radius 70, damage 1.0). Stuck landing: 30 / 26 / 22 frames.
- Fair rules: a fighter standing on the platform jumps the grinder (13 px/frame is slower than the floor run); a fighter on the floor is safe from the rail but not from the landing, and dodges that by moving after the lock.

## 7. THE TWO PHASES (what changes)

The engine splits every boss into 3 tiers at 66% and 33% of its HP. Nine's DESIGN has two acts: act 1 (tier 1) and act 2 (tiers 2 and 3). Tier 3 is only a tempo bump, no new rules, so there is no third design to build.
- ACT 1, "Totally Tubular" (100% to 66%). Moves: TUBULAR! (board), CLEANUP CREW!, BOOYAH!, GRIND!. Gaps 80 (x1.2 = 96 frames). Learn the commit-and-punish rhythm. The first act is short on purpose.
- ACT 2, "Not Radical" (66% to 33%; game-text name, taken from Nine's own line). The engine's phase card, flash, shake and 20 frames of invulnerability happen at the threshold; Nine's own beat waits for the next idle (a threshold crossed mid-run must not break the commit). The beat: Nine stops and cries (the NoStylehaha render, 40 frames), the planet behind cracks into five pieces, the lights go out. New: the motorcycle form, STONE-TO-BOOT!, the crew's platform lane, BOOYAH! as a pair, the +1 hop of everything. Gaps 66 (x1.2 = 79 frames); speeds as above.

## 10. WHAT COULD GO WRONG

Engine limits (these apply to all nine bosses, most are not Nine's alone):
- `bossPaced`, the picker, the one-attack-at-a-time watch, the gap pace and `awardBossCleared` all key off `BOSS_ROSTER` rows (line 27657: `BOSS_ROSTER.some(r => r.attack === s.attack)`). A rush-only list needs `bossPaced` and a few helpers to read both lists. Putting Nine into `BOSS_ROSTER` instead would add him to the normal 12-boss gauntlet, the item boss's random roll (line 27230) and the loop that ends when Four falls: do not.
- The engine puts up a yellow banner at every wind-up: `banner(bossTelName(s), 700, 'boss')` (updateBossAttack, about line 27506). The owner's rule for the rush is no move-name popups, so that line needs `if(name)` and Nine's names left empty. One line, shared by all nine bosses.
- The +50% exposure is a line in `damageSummon`; the phase beat has to be queued to the next idle (`onBossPhaseChange` runs the instant a threshold is crossed); `damageSummon` adds `s.vx` shoves on every hit, which Nine's scripted runs must ignore.
- Online: a client draws only the snapshot, so Nine's own state lives in one small bag of plain numbers (`s._nz`: move, step, frame counter, lock points) in the summons' `net` slot, and everything that moves is either the body (drawn from the bag and `hazardT`) or a shot with a `shape` key (boards, shards, boot, bike as carriers, as Two's sun is). The snapshot rule is the existing one; no live-relay work, and none should be done.
- The CPU: fighters that are bots dodge shots by reading projectiles; I did not run it, so a run that is a carrier shot with `bossAtk` may or may not read right to them. Needs one playtest with bots.
- Stage unknowns I could not check: whether the rush stage scrolls (a long run needs the camera to follow or at least not lose Nine), whether it has edges or voids (the boot and shrapnel rules assume a middle), how many platforms it has (GRIND! needs one 360 px or more). The picker handles a missing platform; a missing floor line does not.

Fairness and stuck spots:
- A run can end with Nine lying on a fighter who stands at the end spot: the hit circle passes through first, so that fighter has taken the hit already; the body has no contact damage. Nine's body cannot trap anyone (no push).
- Falling moves land on the surface under the fighter, so a fighter on a thin edge gets a landing that is half on air: clamp to the platform's span as `twoSunClampX` does for the sun.
- A fighter who hides on a platform is hit by the crew's lane, the boot and the ollie landing, and by the grind itself: four of five reach a ledge.
- Nine on top of a platform: only GRIND! and the landings put Nine there; the move state machine must always return Nine to the floor line (clamp Nine's y to the floor after any step), or a knocked Nine could stick at ledge height.
- A threshold crossed in the middle of a run: the engine sets 20 frames of invulnerability for Nine at once; the act-2 beat waits; make sure an open exposure window is not lost if the threshold fires in it.
- Two fighters at once: Nine locks the picker's target, the other fighter standing in the lane is hit too (one id). Good.
- The multiplier and a long fight: with 1,400 HP the +50% is the difference between a boss you learn and a boss you endure; if it is cut, shorten Nine's gaps rather than HP.
- Honesty notes: poses were classified without looking; the number set is a first draft (no test suite exists and the balance harness cannot run Nine until it is built); the human fight length is unknown.
