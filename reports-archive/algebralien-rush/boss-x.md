Written 2026-11-02 for the "pipis" event. Read-only study of the build at `ii-dlc` (commit a316e32; line numbers below are as of that
commit and drift when merges land: the file moved by 177 lines while I was reading). Nothing in the game was edited. Everything in
CAPITALS (move names, phase names) is game text I propose, not canon. Wiki facts are paraphrased; the single quote is marked.

## 1. Which X

The wiki has one X, and it is all three things the brief names: "X" (BFDI wiki, page `X`) is a Variable, a letter rather than a number, and an
Algebralien ("Variable (Algebralien)", legal name X Variable). They were the co-host of Battle for BFB from the first episodes,
temporarily the main host in BFB 9-10, co-host again in BFB 16-17, then a chef at the Hotel in TPOT. So "an Algebralien X", "a number
character X" (X is not a number: the 2,763 page uses exactly this to say X outranks it) and "the BFB host X" are the same being. Their
value as a math variable is 7 in the 2008 short, and it changes in later material.

Do not confuse it with the Inanimate Insanity wiki: its page `X` is a redirect to MePhoneX, a different character (and one the game already cut from MePhone4's
design, docs/LEDGER.md row G46). I searched the II wiki for an Algebralien X and found nothing.

One more fact that matters: the game already says Four and Two are "the only Algebraliens in the game" (comment near the One fight,
~line 39650). X is the third.

## 2. Canon (what I read, and what it says)

### 2.1 What X is like
- Gentle, nervous, social, dependent on Four; friendlier than Four to the contestants. A bit ignorant (reads the votes backwards, thanks the
  ground for catching someone), messes up under pressure, cries when Four is cruel. Likes baskets (about 40 of them), Four, patty cake;
  dislikes being parted from Four, getting burned, messing up, Screechy.
- Can be pushed to the limit: in BFB 28 they misread Teardrop's shaking head as not caring and vaporize her with lasers from their eyes.
  In TPOT they are meaner (they zap raiders in the Bake-Off) but still the kinder of the two hosts. They rampage against the Hotel (windows) when Clock is
  sent away, but, unlike Four, not at the contestants.
- The one quote I use (X, "Getting Teardrop to Talk", per the X page): "Yayay, so much value!"

### 2.2 What X can do (the X page's "Powers" list, plus episodes)
Flight and hovering (floats up into the sky and stares at the viewers before announcing a challenge: BFB 1 and BFB 11), teleportation (pops), rapid
construction (built a challenge station in 3 s), regeneration (bandages), pain resistance, shape-shifting, heat resistance (lava, magma) but
high flammability (burns in BFB 19-20, 28; ignites themselves on purpose with a snap in BFB 28 and Chapter Complete), G-force resistance (hurled at
the Earth's core at 2,763 mph in Return of the Rocket Ship, broke through the crust), strength, telekinesis and thermoregulation with a finger snap,
super speed, recovery (revives the dead: botched on Purple Face in BFB 28, who came back glitchy and was snapped away), deletion (snap) and
de-spawning (eye lasers, the same family as Four's zappies), self-cloning (Outbreak At Stake), paint from the hand (tags people in The Power
of Four), portal creation (implied). Sometimes takes the shape of an arrow. Wiki trivia: after the 2025 meetup X is the strongest Algebralien; the only power One did not steal.

### 2.3 Items, places, scenes this design uses
- Baskets: brown bowl, two white ropes, a silver propeller; X's. Their contents, X says, are air. Much bigger inside than out: in Four Goes Too Far
  X hid under one and the Losers hid under it too. They can carry things, drop, and fly.
- The 500-step platform (grey stairs, BFB colour "Muled Wine"): in Getting Teardrop to Talk X stands at the top of it, about to lose grip of their baskets;
  X also floats up before announcing. In BFB 11 it is the challenge (climb, press the button at the top).
- The Power of Four: X announces the challenge is Hide & Seek, is a seeker, boasts about being good at it, tags with paint.
- Return of the Rocket Ship: X hurtling through the Earth for treasure at 2,763 mph.
- Getting Teardrop to Talk (BFB 1) stinger: X puts the baskets on and the propellers spin them around while they scream, suspended in the air.
- BFB 28: snap despawn, lasers, ignition (X's burning line there is a joke about BFB 20).

## 3. Place in the rush, HP and how long it takes

- Difficulty position: 6 of 9 (hardest-but-three). HP 2,100 on the owner's curve: 2.1x Two (1,000), 1.75x Four (1,200), 0.875x One (2,400), 0.7x 2,763 (3,000).
  Hit sizes stay a boss hit (`bossDmg()`: 22% on a fighter at the base, times whatever the rush sets) times the fractions below: "harder in dodging, not
  harder in damage or speed" (the owner's rule, ledger H17). X's difficulty comes from patterns that cover a big floor, not from big hits.
- Length. scripts/boss-rush-balance.json (Round 17, solo bots, wins only): Four 510 HP cleared in 63.7 s, Two 427 HP in 63.7 s, MePhone4 382 HP in 53 s:
  about 7 to 8 HP a second per bot. X at 2,100 HP: about 280 s (4.5 min) for one bot; a human or a team will be faster, and the signature's bounty (below,
  about a quarter of his HP) cuts it. Expect 3 to 5 minutes alone; X is about four Fours long (2,100 against Four's 510 in the normal rush). At about 4 s a turn that is 45 to 75 turns,
  so every attack needs a random element (section 6) or it repeats 9 or 10 times. For the whole rush the nine HPs sum to 17,200, about 38 minutes of
  bot time at this rate: not X's problem, but the lead should know.

## 4. The arena: THE 500-STEP PLATFORM (new, simple)

Why: it is X's own place (BFB 1: X waits on top of it for their baskets; the owner also named it). It adds the one thing a big flat floor lacks: height.

- Key `x500`. The sky is a bright grassland day (pale blue to white; a day sky on purpose: the host's cheerful half), clouds on (an open sky, not in BOSS_ARENA_ROOFED).
- The stage is DRESSED at spawn, the way `springyDressArena` does it (worldPlats pushes, flagged `_x`, filtered out in the ending sweep): a stepped pyramid
  in the middle, symmetric, five one-way platforms (pass-through, so nobody gets wedged): a top landing 290 px over the floor (the button,
  drawn on it, 190 px wide) and two steps on each side at 85 and 175 px. Sized in pixels, not fractions, so the rush's larger stage simply has
  more open floor on both sides, where cells, rain and dash matter.
- The backdrop (drawArenaDecor `if(key==="x500")`): the rest of the 500 steps rise behind the pyramid out the top of the screen as a long
  grey stair in perspective, grass hills at the horizon, a few distant baskets drifting with turning propellers. Ground: grey flagstone (the wiki says grey in text and
  Muled Wine, a dark mauve-grey, in the infobox: use the infobox colour at low saturation and check it by eye).
- Phase 2 swaps the key to `x500sun` the way Four's phase 3 swaps to `fourest` (a client only takes a key it has, so BOTH keys go in BOSS_ARENA_SKY): the
  sky crossfades over 90 frames to hot orange and a big sun comes close ("A Taste of Space": X burns because the sun is closer), a heat shimmer, embers rising in the backdrop.
- No arena hazard entry: the embers X drips are the hazard (the owner's "if it makes sense for a hazard, reduce boss difficulty and add a hazard").

## 6. The fight

### 6.1 The grammar (how the house's tells work here)
X follows the shared body-tell system (`BOSS_TELLS`, `BOSS_POSE`, ~line 73536): a count of beats (the same every time, learnable like a dance), then the
body gathers into a pose, lit in a colour, and flickers white for its last 8 frames. No pips, lanes, rings, reticles, arrows or boxes. The only things
the world shows are the ones the house allows: a real shadow under what falls, dust and debris, a glow at the source, the floor cracking where it will
burst. Every move starts the way X starts a challenge in the show: X floats up and stares at you for a beat. Every part of a turn shares ONE attack id,
so a fighter takes at most one boss hit from a move ("Harder, same damage"). Anything that appears grows in over 12 frames. No move-name banners
(X's `telname` slot returns an empty name, and the one `banner(bossTelName(s), 700, 'boss')` call in updateBossAttack needs a one-line guard to skip an empty name; the existing twelve still use
their banners); the only words are the engine's BOSS: X! and PHASE 2 cards, and at most one designed sign (the red button on the top step: see the questions).

Movement (xMove/xIdle, copied from Two's pattern): X floats 70 to 230 px over the floor, bobbing, on the side of you he is already on, 260 px away (220 in phase 2),
following slowly; he pops (shrinks away, appears elsewhere with a ring) to where a move begins when that is more than 300 px away. Fliers and teleporters
are what a larger stage needs: nobody outruns a thing that can be anywhere.

Engine units: wind-ups below are in engine units and a frame count after `BOSS_TEL_PACE` (I propose X at 1.25 like Four and Springy); gaps are before
`BOSS_PACE` (x1.2). At 60 frames a second.

### 6.2 The attacks

| | BASKET RAIN! | SNAP! | ROCKET DIVE! | ARROW DASH! | BURNING! (phase 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canon | the baskets that fly and carry; the pile of baskets (BFB 1, 28) | the finger snap: deletion and despawn (BFB 28) | hurled through the Earth at 2,763 mph (Return of the Rocket Ship) | X as an arrow; super speed (Lick Your Way to Freedom, The Four is Lava, Who Stole Donut's Diary?) | sets himself on fire (BFB 19-20, 28; Chapter Complete) |
| Wind-up (units / frames) | 44 / 55 | 48 / 60 | 40 / 50 | 36 / 45 | 36 / 45 |
| Shape | falling area, one basket per fighter plus a fill | floor columns that delete in a checker | slam on one fighter, then floor waves | cross-stage charge along one row | panic flight, embers, a damage window |
| Picker tags | ground, spread | ground, platform | close, ground | far | air, far |
| Damage (x a boss hit) | 0.9 | 1.0 | 1.0 impact, 0.7 wave | 1.0 | 0.6 body, 0.3 ember, 0.25 patch |

Gap after a move: 110 frames in phase 1, 84 in phase 2 (x1.2 = 132 and 101 frames: 2.2 s and 1.7 s). Compare Two 140 / 110 / 95.

**1. BASKET RAIN!**
- Tell: X rises toward the top of the screen, arms up, a warm white glow on his hands ('rise', 2 beats), a rising whirr (sound). Over his last 12 frames a pile of baskets
  grows in above him, propellers turning. Source glow only.
- Fire: the baskets fan out into a row across the stage at a high lane over 22 frames. One basket per living fighter follows that fighter's x until 14 frames before
  the drop, then locks; the rest fill the row evenly (about 215 px apart: five on a 1,100 px floor, eight or nine on a big one), never closer than 70 px to the edge.
  A real shadow under each basket grows in over 12 frames. The drop: the propeller pops off, the basket falls (gravity 0.6, about 35 frames from 560 px), and HITS WHILE FALLING
  (radius 34, 0.9 of a boss hit, knocked 8 aside and 9 up); it lands with a hollow thud and wicker chips (impact kit) and lies upside down as a low one-way step for 90 frames,
  then its propeller comes back on and it lifts off.
- Phase 2: a second row 40 frames later, placed where you stand now.
- Random element: which fighters lock, which fill slots are used (always leaving a gap at least one body wide), which side the row enters from.
- Fair because: you always have a shadow (12-frame grow-in plus the fall), and the gaps are always there.

**2. SNAP!**
- Tell: X floats to the middle of the pyramid's height, one hand raised with the four fingers pinched (a small glint at the hand: a code-drawn overlay), body tipped back
  ('tilt'), THREE beats, the same every time: one, two, three, snap (a click and a one-frame white flash).
- Fire: the floor is split into columns 150 px wide (only the floor's span, never the void). The snap makes one half of the columns glitch (static noise with a colour split grows in over
  12 frames and the floor under it flickers), 44 frames in phase 1 (38 in phase 2); then those columns are DELETED: for 6 frames anything inside a column, up to 360 px high, takes 1.0 of a boss
  hit and is launched mostly UP (-14) with a tiny push toward the middle (never toward an edge). The columns show black static for 24 frames (harmless) and fade back. A second snap 28
  frames later does the other half. Phase 2 adds a third snap (the first half again, shifted a column when the count is odd).
- The first half is the one with the most fighters in it, so standing still is what gets hit; the safe columns are always the next ones over (at most 150 px away: 24 frames at
  the run speed of 6.4, 43 at a slow 3.5). Cell width and glitch time are the two knobs; tune them against the slowest runner in the roster.
- The top step (290 px) is under the 360 px ceiling, so the pyramid is not a refuge; the air above 360 px is (that is the 'air' stance the other moves punish).
- Why not eye lasers: the BFB 28 despawn used lasers, but One's kit already has EYE LASERS! and Four ZAPPIES!, and One is the very next boss. X's despawn is the snap.

**3. ROCKET DIVE!**
- Tell: one beat, X crouches, then shoots up off the top of the screen (a streak). While he is gone: a real shadow grows in on the floor under the fighter he marked (the nearest; it
  rotates among fighters), follows that fighter until 14 frames before impact, then locks; a bright glow grows at the top edge over the spot (the source).
- Fire: about 46 frames later he comes down in 18 frames (about 36 px a frame) along a steep line onto the lock (hits while falling). Impact radius 110: 1.0 of a boss hit, launch -15 up and 6
  out, a floor scar, shake 12. Then two low shockwaves run each way (36 px high, 8 px a frame, 460 px far, 0.7 of a hit: jump them). The `bossAtk` id is shared, so a fighter eats at most one.
- The window: X is stuck head-first in the floor for 70 frames (the same render turned upside down, legs kicking), cannot attack, takes hits normally; then he pops out. Phase 2: no stuck window
  after the first dive; a second dive 30 frames later onto the next fighter's lock, and the window comes after the second.
- A wink: his move can say "2,763 mph" in the comments, nowhere on screen.

**4. ARROW DASH!**
- Tell: X zips to the edge farther from the marked fighter, on that fighter's row (floor, ledge or jump height, clamped to 40 to 260 px over the floor, so the top step at 290 is the refuge from it), the row
  following the fighter until 14 frames before the dash, then locked (as MePhone4's glove row does). He tilts into an arrow pointing across the stage ('lunge', 2 beats, squashed long and thin), a bright
  yellow glow, a short afterimage tail behind him; the last 14 frames flicker white.
- Fire: across the whole stage at 30 px a frame (37 frames on 1,100 px, about 60 on 1,800), afterimages trailing; 1.0 of a boss hit, knocked along the path 14 and up -6; he leaves the screen and pops back to his float.
- Phase 2: a second pass back on a different row (next up or down), 20 frames after the first.
- Why it matters on a large stage: it cannot be outrun, only jumped or ducked by row.

**5. BURNING! (opens phase 2)**
- Tell: X snaps once (one beat) and bursts into flame (the `xburn` render, orange glow, a crown of flames), trembling ('shake' pose) 20 frames, eyes wide. In phase 2 he is already alight, so this
  is a FLARE (the flames flare up and he bolts), not an ignition.
- Fire: a fixed flight, a figure of eight across 85% of the stage width over 150 frames (3 learned variants, mirrored by which side he starts from), at up to 11 px a frame, 90 to 260 px
  over the floor, so he crosses at head height and over the pyramid. His body hits: 0.6 of a boss hit, knocked 12, 120 frames of grace (the softened contact Evil Leafy has). He sheds an
  ember every 9 frames (7 in phase 2): it falls (hits while falling, 0.3), and lies burning on the floor for 90 frames, a patch of radius 22 that hits for 0.25 once every 40 frames; at most 8 alive.
- THE WINDOW: while he panics he is in pain: damage dealt to him is x1.25 for the whole flight (a bonus read from his hp change each frame: no edit to the many places that damage a boss). He is also
  at head height all over the arena, so there is a hit to be had. He is doused in a cloud of steam at the end in phase 1 and he does not put it out again in phase 2.
- Phase 2: two passes (out and back, the second lower).

### 6.3 THE SIGNATURE: HIDE AND SEEK!

What makes X different from every other boss in the rush. Two flies and pops, Four slithers and screams, Nine to 2,763 do their own things; none of them hides. X is the host of Hide & Seek
in The Power of Four, hid under his baskets in Four Goes Too Far, and the baskets are bigger on the inside than the outside. The fight turns into a cups-and-ball game, and finding him is how you hurt him most.

- When: every fifth turn (the picker's `force` hook, the same one the Announcer's forced move uses) and never in a row with itself; X's first turn in phase 2 is BURNING! (`first` hook).
- Tell: one beat, X crouches ('crouch'), and over the last 12 frames three baskets grow in around him, propellers turning. He ducks into one (a quick shrink), and the sound is a bunch of propellers.
- The shuffle: three baskets in phase 1, four in phase 2, hang in a row 100 to 180 px over the floor and swap places like cups, one swap every 50 frames (3 swaps) in phase 1, every 36 (5 swaps) in phase 2.
  Every basket is on the same path function of the swap list, so a basket is always where the list says; nothing is random after the start. The baskets then settle and bob, drifting a few px toward the nearest fighter
  so a far fighter is not made to cross a big stage.
- Which one is X: the real one (X is the boss summon itself, drawn as a basket and shrunk to the basket's size while he is inside: the hurtbox must match) hangs 6 px lower (his weight) and twitches
  a pixel now and then; its propeller whirs a little faster. The decoys are hostile adds (the pattern of Steve Cobs's MeTags: `hostile:true, hover:true`), 1 HP.
- Pay-off, the bounty: the first hit on the real basket (read as X's hp dropping while hidden) FINDS him: he tumbles out dizzy (the `xoh` render) for 100 frames, cannot attack,
  takes his normal hit plus a flat 2.5% of his max HP at once (52 of 2,100), and hits count x1.5 during the stun. About ten of these in a fight is about a quarter of his HP, which
  is also what keeps a 2,100 HP fight under five minutes. The percent is a tunable (2%, 3%, 4%).
- Cost of a wrong guess: a decoy bursts in a puff of air (the contents) that nicks the nearest fighter within 90 px (0.35 of a boss hit, a light push, no ring-out) and is gone. Guessing is cheap but
  not free, and the hunt is short:
- The clock: if X is not found within 330 frames (270 in phase 2) of the shuffle ending, he pops out annoyed and every basket still hanging drops on the nearest fighter in a BASKET RAIN (the same code,
  shadows included). Failing the game is not a free turn.
- Ends: found, or timed out; the decoys are removed; X's radius is restored.

### 6.4 Phases (2)

- THE HOST, above 50%: the grin render, bright day sky, four attacks and the shuffle, the numbers above in their phase 1 column.
- PUSHED TO THE LIMIT, below 50% (BFB 28 is the wiki's example of X being pushed too far): the beat (below), then X is alight for good: the `xburn` render, a hot sky and a close sun, and a passive ember drip (one every
  45 frames, a patch of 60 frames at 0.2) as his arena hazard. Every attack gets its phase 2 line: RAIN a second row, SNAP a third snap, DIVE a second dive, DASH a second pass, the shuffle one more basket and quicker swaps,
  gaps 84, BURNING! becomes available (and opens the phase).
- The beat (about 60 frames): he freezes mid-air in the `xoh` pose, a quiet beat, a snap (sound), flames burst over him, the engine's PHASE 2 card and its 24 puffs, the sky crossfades,
  `s.invuln` for 20 frames (the engine's).
- Engine touch: the engine cuts phases at 66% and 33% (a 3-phase system in updateBossAttack). Two phases means a one-line per-boss cut table (`PHASE_CUTS`, X: 50%), or X accepts the three and treats 3 as 2
  (the PHASE 3 card would still show). I recommend the cut table: 3 lines.

## 7. Intro and defeat

Intro (about 100 frames, from BFB 1: X stands at the top of the 500 steps about to lose grip of their baskets): X is on the top landing, wobbling, holding a bunch of baskets whose propellers lift him, bobbing, a hand's
width off the step; the baskets pull him up and he lets go; the baskets float off up out of the screen (they come back for the rain); X drops down to float over the pyramid, grinning (`xhappy`, if fetched). The engine's BOSS: X!
card arrives with him; his first attack waits as the engine's first wait does (120 frames). No words. (One optional beat, cheap: a very faint dimming of the sky as the fight starts, since One is next and X is the one who
freaks out when she returns; it does nothing and may be cut.)

## 9. What could go wrong

Unfair spots
- SNAP! and slow runners (tune cell width and glitch time against the slowest runner). Snap launches are up and toward the middle: an edge column must not ring someone out.
- DIVE and BURNING! near the ledge: the dive's launch is mostly up (a ring-out from a side launch at the edge would feel cheap); embers must not stack on the spawn spots (cap 8, and respawn grace skips them).
- ARROW DASH! on the top step: it is meant to be a refuge from it (and it is punished by the rain and the snap); if a CPU parks there, the picker tags (platform) will pick the snap more.
- RAIN fill leaving no gap at the edges (the 70 px margin and "gap at least one body wide" rule are for this).
- Hide and Seek: a Final Smash or any area move can hit every basket in one frame (finds him at once: fine), and a fighter who cannot reach 180 px can still shoot or jump; baskets drift toward fighters.
- A fighter killed during the lock: retarget to the nearest living fighter (the Four/Two helpers `*Nearest` do this).

Stuck spots
- A landed basket as a pass-through step cannot wedge anyone (I chose one-way on purpose; solid, like Two's block steps, could).
- X stuck in the floor after a dive while a fighter stands in him: bosses have no body collision, so nothing jams; the stuck pose must put the head BELOW `groundY`, never above a platform.
- The shuffle ending: the hurtbox radius is shrunk for the shuffle and must be restored on every exit (found, timeout, death, ending); store it in `_hz` and restore in `xTick` if a turn ends oddly.
- If every fighter dies mid-shuffle the engine ends the run, and `bossRushCheck` removes hostile adds when the boss dies.

Engine, CPU and online limits (each one a decision the lead should make once for all nine bosses)
1. BOSS_ROSTER membership. `bossPaced`, the picker (`bossPicks`), the gap and the wind-up pace all test `BOSS_ROSTER.some(r => r.attack === s.attack)`. The rush's roster rows must satisfy it: either the
   rush rows live in BOSS_ROSTER (then the normal Boss Rush would spawn them: it walks the whole list) or `bossPaced` is widened to a second list. This affects X and every Algebralien boss.
2. Outside-the-slot lines X needs (as Springy's and Steve Cobs's did): the phase cut table; one line in `updateSummons` (`if(s.type==="xbasket"){ xBasketStep(s); continue; }`, like MeTag's `cobsMinionStep`),
   and the decoy basket's draw and its net row (a type name and a few numbers: `serializeState` already sends `hostile`, `_dl`, `_telKind`; a client draws a basket from `type`, `x`, `y` and the clock).
3. One attack at a time: the engine holds the next turn until every shot of the last is gone. The shuffle has no shots, so the picker's `busy` hook must say "the shuffle is going", or the next wind-up starts under it.
4. CPU: bots treat hostile adds and the boss as targets (`isAssistFoe`), so they will pop decoys, which is correct and finds X in about 3 s: bots are good at Hide and Seek. The 330-frame clock makes sure no hunt can stall.
5. Online: everything X draws is the snapshot as the other bosses' is (`_hz` bag of numbers read against `hazardT`, shots, summons with x and y). The shuffle plan (slots and swap list) goes in `_hz`; nothing is random on the
   client. The live relay is not touched; the guest/host pair was not run (no building here).
6. HP: spawnBossRushBoss computes `hp*1.5*(1+(n-1)*0.6)` for the normal rush. The 2,100 and the bounty (a percent of `maxHp`) must be set by the rush's own spawn so they scale together.
7. Damage windows are read from hp deltas in `xTick` (the "pain" x1.25 and the found bonus). A hit that arrives through some other path still counts, since it only needs hp to drop. A shield that returns hp (none today) would be read as a heal: ignored.
8. Phase text: the engine's PHASE card names X's phase through `bossPhaseName`; the second phase's name is game text and must be added.
9. Variety: 45 to 60 turns of six moves need the random elements above, or the fight reads as a loop.
