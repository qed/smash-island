# X's juice box: the owner's own attack, three ways to build it (2026-11-02)

The owner typed into X's attack box: "juice box: stream of juice." This file designs it for approval: three genuinely different builds, one recommendation, and the one-line versions for a question box. Read-only study. Nothing in the game was edited, committed, pushed or run, nothing was downloaded (the wiki was read as raw wikitext text in my own browser-pane tab, closed afterwards), and no option exists in the game yet. Sources in this folder: `owner-rules.md`, `attack-picks.md` (the owner's picks and general rule), `spec.md` (sections 8 to 10), `boss-x.md`, `attack-options-x.md` and the other attack-options files (the shapes already taken). Everything in CAPITALS (move names) is game text, not canon. X is "they/them" in internal text (spec section 12), so I mostly write "X".

The owner's general rule applies to all three: "remember to give bosses phases, and ALL attacks should get harder by phase, like cobs." X has two phases (cut once at 50%): THE HOST (above 50%) and PUSHED TO THE LIMIT (below 50%, X alight, hot sky). Damage stays the same in both phases ("harder in dodging, not harder in damage": ledger H17, quoted in `boss-x.md`).

## 1. Is the juice box canon for X? Yes for the box, no for the stream

What I read (BFDI wiki, raw wikitext, 2026-11-02):
- The wiki has a Juice Box ITEM (section "Juice Box" of `List of minor items in Battle for BFDI`; the page `Juice Box (item)` redirects there). Its infobox: owner X, used on Four, contents juice, first appearance "The Escape from Four", effect "cools down Four". Two versions of the box exist, an orange one and an apple one (the apple box carries the letters AJ).
- "The Escape from Four" (episode page and transcript): Four is in pain from Firey Jr.'s fire. X comes in and offers an orange juice box to make Four feel better (X's line in the transcript: "Four, here's a juice box to help you feel better. Four...?"); Four does not respond. Later X pops in again (a "woop" sound) with another box, an apple one, and asks whether Four would like another; Four takes it as X leaving for Two's show and tells X to go. The `Four` page lists "burn intolerance", curable only by feeling better, "often via a Juice Box". The `Four and X` page and `X/Coverage` repeat the scene.
- Nearby juice canon: tangerine juice (a Cake at Stake prize, `Tangerine juice` page) appears from Four's hand in "Four Goes Too Far" and turns the contestants who are safe orange; in that scene X changes the vote count and is asked who is safe. A hand that makes juice, and a juice that marks you, is the closest canon to a stream.
- NOT found: any scene of X squirting, spraying or pouring juice at anyone. In canon the box is only ever OFFERED, as comfort. X's own page has no mention of juice (I searched its raw text). So the STREAM is game text. The joke is an inversion: the thing X uses to cool a burning friend, aimed at the player.
- The best canon ties: the box is the show's cure for fire, and X is flammable and alight in phase 2; and the two boxes (orange first, then "another", an apple one) give the phase colours (orange in phase 1, apple gold in phase 2, and in phase 2 the second box in the other hand).
- A BFDI Mini Again contestant is also called Juice Box and shares the orange box's art. It is a different thing: this attack uses only the item, drawn in code (a plain carton, a drawn fruit, a bendy straw, no face, no limbs, and no lettering: the canon box's AJ letters are left off by the no-words rule).
- Inanimate Insanity wiki: its `X` page is only a redirect to MePhoneX (a different character, as `boss-x.md` section 1 already says), and there is no Juice Box page there (two page-name tries returned 404). I did not search it further. Nothing from that wiki ties X to a juice box.

Could not confirm: that X ever fires juice; the episode itself (I read the wiki text and the transcript, I did not watch it).

## 2. Rules, yardsticks and the parts all three share

Rules kept (spec section 10, quoted from the brief): the tell is in the body (a pose, a glow, a beat count, a 14-frame gather, a white flicker in the last 8 frames); no telegraph lines, rings, banners or move-name popups (X's `telname` is empty); the only world marks are a real shadow under what falls, dust and a glow at the source; anything that appears grows in over 12 frames; falling things hit while they fall; one attack id per turn (a fighter takes at most ONE hit from the move); nothing above one boss hit (22%); no contact damage; no strobe; launches go up or toward the middle; stage 2,400 px wide with solid end walls, a 650 px leash (X relocates to 300 to 500 px from you), the 500-Step pyramid (steps 85 and 175 px, a top landing 290 px).

Yardsticks (from the Four and X option files): a run is 6.4 px a frame (roster 5.4 to 7.2; I use 5 as the slowest); the weakest full jump rises 107 px, a full one 126, a short hop 54; jump gravity 0.6 a frame squared, so time above a height h is computed from that. My assumptions, to be read from the game when built: a standing fighter is about 100 px tall (the Six file's yardstick) and has a body radius of about 34 px for clearance.

Shared by all three (built once, about 35 lines): the carton and the straw (code-drawn); the drop (radius 20, growing from 8 over the first 12 frames of any squirt); a drop that touches a fighter hits (knock 8 px a frame toward the stage middle and 9 up) and pops; drops pop on the first surface they meet and leave nothing on the floor; all drops of a turn share one attack id. Colour: orange in phase 1 (the first box), pale apple gold in phase 2 (the second), with a dark amber outline and a white highlight line so it reads on the pale day sky and the hot orange one, and colour is never the only tell. The glow at the straw is pale lime, not orange (BURNING! owns orange and X is alight in phase 2).

## 4. The three options

### 1. GUSH!: a lobbed stream that hops from place to place

- Looks like (the tell): X rises to 170 px over the floor on their side and faces you, the box held overhead in both hands, the straw angled out and down toward you. Two squeezes, 20 frames apart: the carton crumples and two small pale drops (radius 6, harmless) dribble off the straw. Then a 14-frame gather: the carton swells, a pale lime glow grows at the straw tip, and the body flickers white for the last 8 frames. Wind-up 54 frames. Then the jet: a thick orange stream droops from the straw to the floor in an arc (a flight of 30 frames whatever the distance, so the sideways speed is the distance over 30: about 14 px a frame at 420 px; it lands at 45 degrees or steeper). Each drop casts its real shadow, so the foot shows where the shadows bunch. The straw stays pointed at the foot, which is the tell for where the next place will be.
- Does: a squirt is 22 frames of one drop a frame. The FOOT is a column of falling drops for 22 frames, starting 30 frames after the squirt starts, plus a splash circle of radius 44 for 6 frames where each drop lands. Between squirts the stream shuts off for 12 frames while the straw swings to the next place (a thin dribble only, harmless). Each place is LOCKED 14 frames before its squirt: x = your x + lead, where lead = your velocity x 44 frames (14 + 30), capped at 250 px, kept 60 px or more from an end wall and on your side of X. With more than one fighter the places go to the nearest living fighter and rotate. Your centre must be 78 px (44 + 34) from the foot to be clear. Phase 1: 3 squirts, 144 frames from the first beat to the last emission, 174 until the last drop lands.
- Dodge: be 78 px or more from the foot when it lands: 16 frames at 5 px a frame, 13 at a run, and you have 44 frames from each lock. The lock reads what you were doing: standing is hit where you stand, running is hit AHEAD of you (the lead), so stop, reverse or step sideways after the lock; doing the same thing twice loses. The cheap answer is IN: the arc stays above head height (about 100 px) for roughly its first half, so running toward X passes under it and puts you next to the boss.
- Why running or a wall does not beat it: the foot is placed ahead of a runner and re-placed 3 to 5 times (a run in a line is hit by the first or second), the stream reaches about 800 px so no spot on the stage is out of range, and the places clamp to 60 px from the end wall, so a fighter pinned at a wall sits under a foot and must step toward the middle. The steps are no refuge: drops pop on the first surface and the splash covers the step.
- Damage: 0.8 (17.6%), once per turn (all squirts share the id).
- PHASE ESCALATION. Phase 1 (THE HOST): the numbers above, orange. Phase 2 (PUSHED TO THE LIMIT, apple gold): FIVE squirts of 18 frames with 8-frame swings (more, faster); flight 24 frames and lead 38 frames (less time, a longer lead: tighter); one squeeze instead of two, so the tell is 34 frames; and the fifth squirt is a PINCER, the second box in X's other hand: two feet 250 px apart centred where you stood at the lock (no lead), so the safe spot is the MIDDLE, a 94 px slot (your centre 78 px from each foot). The first four punish staying, the last punishes fleeing (chained). Phase 2 runs 180 frames. If X ever got a third phase: a sixth squirt and 4 frames off every swing.
- Exposure window: none. X stays hittable at 170 px (their body spans about 78 to 262 px up, and the weakest jump reaches 107).
- Canon: the juice box (X's item, "The Escape from Four"); orange box first, "another" box (apple) second, and orange tangerine juice that comes from Four's hand in "Four Goes Too Far". The lob, the chain and the pincer are game text.
- Size: about 95 lines (state machine 35, the lock and lead 15, drop step and shadows 25, the second box 20), plus the shared 35.
- Risks: STUCK: the target dies mid-chain, so the next place retargets to the nearest living fighter and the move ends if none is left; every squirt has a fixed frame count and the turn has a 240-frame cap (the longest is 180), so nothing waits on something that never comes. INFINITE: none, there is no loop. UNFAIR: a pincer slot of 94 px is the number to tune against the slowest runner; the lead cap keeps a foot from landing off the stage. ONLINE: the places, the squirt index and the clock live in the `_hz` bag; each drop is (x, y, vx, vy, age) stepped by one formula; shadows are drawn from a drop's x and the floor; no client randomness.
- Nearest relatives, and why it is not them: BASKET RAIN! and ROCKET DIVE! also come down on a lock, so the kit would have three lock-landers; this one is a stream you watch from the straw to the floor, a CHAIN of places that re-read you, with no steps left behind, no waves and no stuck window. Six's BUBBLES! is a slow homing stream; this does not home.

### 2. SQUIRT!: a long flat jet, low then high, and the box runs dry

- Looks like (the tell): X drops to 90 px over the floor and turns side-on to you, the box in one hand like a pistol, the straw pointing at you. Two squeezes, 18 frames apart (the carton crumples, two harmless drops). Then the pose of the first band, held 14 frames: DIP (X tips forward, the straw at ankle height, the carton low) means a LOW band; LIFT (X leans back, the straw at head height, the carton up) means a HIGH band. The carton swells, a pale lime glow grows at the straw tip, and the body flickers white for the last 8 frames. Wind-up 50 frames. Between bands the straw swings to the next pose in 12 frames: the swing is the tell for the next band, read in the gap.
- Does: a band is 22 frames of one drop a frame, flat and straight at 24 px a frame (30 in phase 2), no gravity, dying at 1,400 px or at the end wall (it does not bounce). LOW covers 0 to 40 px over the floor; HIGH covers 120 to 160 px. The gap between bands is 24 frames. The side is re-read in every gap (X turns in the swing), so crossing over X buys one band at most. A fighter is hit when a band overlaps their body (feet up to about 100 px). The band reaches you distance / speed frames after it starts (500 px away is 21 frames). Phase 1: 3 bands in one of four fixed orders: HIGH-LOW-HIGH, LOW-HIGH-LOW, HIGH-HIGH-LOW, LOW-HIGH-HIGH.
- Dodge: LOW: jump it, or stand on the 85, 175 or 290 step. The weakest full jump (107 px) stays above the 40 px top for about 30 frames, a full one for 34, a short hop (54) for only about 14, so take off 4 to 12 frames before the band reaches you. HIGH: stay on the floor (head about 100 px, the band starts at 120), or be on the 175 or 290 step; do not jump, and do not stand on the 85 step (a body from 85 to 185 meets 120 to 160). After a LOW the next band reaches you 46 frames later (40 in phase 2); the weakest jump is in the air for 38 frames, so a take-off 4 frames early lands 34 frames after the first band arrives: 12 frames clear of the next band in phase 1 and 6 in phase 2, at the worst.
- Why running or a wall does not beat it: the front crosses 1,400 px in 58 frames (47 in phase 2), while a runner covers 370 px (300); it runs the whole stage on X's side and is re-aimed each gap; a wall only stops the jet, and a fighter pinned at it still jumps or stays low.
- Damage: 0.6 (13.2%), one hit per turn (all bands share the id).
- PHASE ESCALATION. Phase 1 (THE HOST): 3 bands, 24 px a frame, gap 24, two squeezes, orange, window 56 frames. Phase 2 (PUSHED TO THE LIMIT, apple gold): FIVE bands (more), 30 px a frame (faster), gap 18 (tighter), one squeeze so the tell is 32 frames (shorter), four fixed orders that never put two LOWs in a row (HIGH-LOW-HIGH-LOW-HIGH, LOW-HIGH-HIGH-LOW-HIGH, HIGH-HIGH-LOW-HIGH-LOW, LOW-HIGH-LOW-HIGH-HIGH: chained), window 42 frames. If X ever got a third phase: a sixth band and a gap of 16.
- Exposure window: after the last band the carton is empty. X pinches it flat, shakes it, a small puff, looks up (the planned `xoh` render): 56 frames in phase 1 and 42 in phase 2, no attacks, damage x1.25 (through the spec's shared exposed state), hanging 90 px over the floor so a floor fighter can jump to them. The window ends on its own timer.
- Canon: the juice box (X's item, its contents are juice, "The Escape from Four"); an empty box is game text, and it suits the wiki's gentle, nervous X who "messes up under pressure" (`boss-x.md` section 2.1). The jet is game text.
- Size: about 85 lines (the pose-driven state machine 30, band emission 20, drop step and hit 20, the dry window 15), plus the shared 35.
- Risks: STUCK: the sequence is an index into a table, the window ends on a timer, and a dead target falls back to the nearest living fighter for the side re-read. INFINITE: none. UNFAIR: the HIGH band catches a jumper and the 85 step is a trap by design (the check must say so); the phase 2 landing margin (6 frames) is the first number to tune, and if it feels short, widen that gap to 20. BEAM FATIGUE: see below. ONLINE: the order index, band clock and side live in `_hz`; drops are stepped by one formula.
- Nearest relatives, and why it is not them: Three's HALLWAY LASER (an instant row beam locked to one row) and One's EYE LASERS; this is a travelling front (the arrival depends on your distance), a two-height beat of 3 to 5 bands, and it ends with the box dry. X's own ARROW DASH! is a body pass on your row at 30 px a frame, once: if both are in the kit the picker must never chain them. Six's BUBBLE BLOWER 7000 (not picked) is a vertical sweep.

### 3. FIZZ!: a shaken carton sprays a fan of arcs with swaying safe slots

- Looks like (the tell): X hovers at 130 px, the box in both hands at chest height, and shakes it hard: three shakes, 18 frames apart (the carton rattles and pale fizz dots hop off it, code-drawn). Then a thumb pops the straw (a pop sound), and for the last 14 frames the carton swells to twice its size, a pale lime glow grows at the straw, and the body flickers white for the last 8. The carton tilts toward the first sway direction, which is always toward the middle of the stage. Wind-up 68 frames.
- Does: five jets (seven in phase 2) leave the straw together and droop to the floor, one drop per 3 frames each (radius 20), a 30-frame flight: a fan of arcs. Feet at c + k x 240 px, k = -2 to 2 (phase 2: c + k x 220, k = -3 to 3), where c is your x at the lock, locked 14 frames before the fan starts, no lead: the foot at k = 0 is on you. A foot hits as in GUSH! (falling drops plus a splash of radius 44; your centre must be 78 px from every foot), so the safe SLOT between two feet is 84 px wide (64 in phase 2). The fan runs 96 frames (one sway period) in phase 1, and the whole fan sways by 80 sin(2 pi t / 96) px (up to 5.2 px a frame). A foot that would land beyond a wall is dropped. Phase 1 total: 68 + 96 + 30 = 194 frames.
- Dodge: step into the nearest slot, 120 px from where you stand (19 frames at 6.4, 24 at 5; you have 14 + 30 = 44 frames), then ride it: the slot sways 80 px each way, you move with it (up to 5.2 px a frame, so every runner can) and it is 84 px wide, so 42 px of slack. Standing still in a slot gets you hit once the sway passes the slack.
- Why running or a wall does not beat it: the fan spans 960 px around you (1,320 in phase 2) and a runner covers 281 px in the 44 frames, so you cannot leave it; a wall removes only the slot on the wall side, and the slot toward the middle always remains.
- Damage: 0.8 (17.6%), once per turn.
- PHASE ESCALATION. Phase 1 (THE HOST): the numbers above, orange, one fan. Phase 2 (PUSHED TO THE LIMIT, apple gold): SEVEN jets (more), spacing 220 so slots shrink to 64 px (tighter), the sway repeats every 84 frames (faster: up to 6.0 px a frame; the slowest runner's lag is about 8 px, inside the 32 px slack), and TWO fans of 60 frames with a 10-frame cut between them, the second re-locked on you (chained); two shakes instead of three, so the tell is 50 frames. Total 50 + 60 + 10 + 60 + 30 = 210 frames. If X ever got a third phase: a third fan.
- Exposure window: none.
- Canon: tangerine juice from a hand marks who is "safe" (`Tangerine juice`, "Four Goes Too Far"; X is in that scene): here the slots are the safe places. The fan and the sway are game text, and shaking a carton to make it spray is not canon.
- Size: about 105 lines (fan and feet 35, the sway and the cut 25, drops and shadows 25, the shake and pop overlay 20), plus the shared 35.
- Risks: STUCK: the fan runs on a timer and the cut re-locks on the nearest living fighter. INFINITE: none. UNFAIR: the 64 px slot of phase 2 is tuned against the slowest runner (the knob is the spacing: 240 gives 84 px again); drop budget is about 77 alive at the peak (cap 100, the lowest life that still reaches the floor). ONLINE: c, the sway clock and the fan index live in `_hz`; the sway is a sine of the plain tick, no client randomness.
- Nearest relatives, and why it is not them: BASKET RAIN! (spaced falling things with gaps, about 215 px) and SNAP! (alternating columns). This is the weakest choice for variety if both are picked, because X would have three spaced-column moves; what differs is arcs from one source you can watch and gaps that MOVE.

## 5. Fairness checks used for all three

- Each clear spot can be reached at 5 px a frame inside the time between the lock and the landing: GUSH! 16 frames to clear against 44 (38 in phase 2); SQUIRT! a take-off window of 4 to 12 frames for the weakest jump; FIZZ! 24 frames to reach a slot against 44.
- Nothing is faster than a run and also unaimed: GUSH! and FIZZ! are placed at a lock (GUSH! also leads your run); SQUIRT! travels 24 to 30 px a frame.
- Nothing makes X unhittable: X hangs at 170 (GUSH!), 90 (SQUIRT!) and 130 px (FIZZ!), and the weakest jump reaches 107.
- One attack id per turn: a fighter takes at most one hit from any of them; the biggest hit is 0.8 (17.6%), inside the 22% cap.
- No ring-outs: every knock is up and toward the stage middle; the walls are solid.
- Nothing stays behind: no puddle, no step, no patch; every drop pops. Drop budget at the peak: GUSH! about 60, SQUIRT! about 45, FIZZ! about 77.
- All starting values (the 14-frame lock, the lead cap, the band length, the slot widths) are tuned by feel, never to a bot.

## 6. Recommendation

SQUIRT! (option 2), with GUSH! (option 1) as the second choice and FIZZ! last.
- It is the most literal answer to "stream of juice": a jet you watch cross the whole screen, which is what the owner pictured.
- It is the one X does not already have: X's picked kit lands things on spots (BASKET RAIN!, ROCKET DIVE!, SNAP!'s columns), crosses once at speed (ARROW DASH!) and flies (BURNING!). A jump-or-stay-low beat is a new verb, and it is the only option with a payoff (the box runs dry, the window opens), which the owner's pattern of rewarding dodges favours.
- It has the cleanest phase ladder (3 bands to 5, 24 px to 30, gap 24 to 18, tell 50 to 32, window 56 to 42) and the smallest drop budget.
- The risk is beam fatigue (X's ARROW DASH!, One's EYE LASERS, Three's HALLWAY LASER sit in bosses 6 to 8). The answers: the picker never chains it with ARROW DASH!; it is slow enough to read (a juice front, not a flash); and if the owner finds it samey after the first playthrough, GUSH! is the swap, since the arc and the chain share only the "lands on a lock" idea with RAIN and DIVE.
- Take FIZZ! only if SNAP! and BASKET RAIN! are cut.

## 7. The one-line versions for the question box (200 characters at most)

1. GUSH! X squirts a drooping arc of juice that lands on you, then flicks to where you dodged, 3 times (5 in phase 2, the last a two-box pincer). Change what you were doing each time.
2. SQUIRT! X fires a long flat jet across the whole stage, low then high on a beat: jump the low ones, stay down for the high ones. Then the box runs dry and X is open to hits.
3. FIZZ! X shakes the box and sprays a fan of juice arcs with safe slots between them that sway side to side. Slide into a slot and ride it. Phase 2: more arcs, narrower slots, a re-aim.
