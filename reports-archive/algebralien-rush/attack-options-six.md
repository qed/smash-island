# Six, Algebralien Boss Rush: attack options (2026-11-02)

Read-only study. Nothing in the game was edited, committed or pushed; no browser was launched (the wiki was read as raw wikitext through `fetch` in an already-open BFDI tab of the built-in pane, without navigating it). The game was read only for yardsticks (`GRAV` 0.62, `MAXVX` 6.4, `JUMP` -12.5, the old pull in `fourByeStep`, `twoPop`, `bossCrack`, `bossFallShadow`). Nothing here was played; every number is a starting value.

The owner's ask: "give me options for the attacks" (the owner chooses in question boxes and likes to pick more than five). Six is the fourth rush boss: health 1,600 plus two bubbles of 120, on the common 2,400 x 860 stage with solid end walls, a 650 px leash, 4 stocks +2 per boss. The director's spec fixes five designed moves for her (section 1); this file adds three new ones, so the owner has eight to pick from.

House rules every option honours (spec section 10): the tell is in the body (a pose, a glow at the source, a beat count, a 12 to 14 frame gather, one 8-frame white flicker), no telegraph lines, rings, reticles, banners or move-name popups; the only world marks are a real shadow under what falls, grit and dust, a glow at the source, a floor crack; anything that appears grows in over 12 frames; falling things hit while they fall; one attack id per turn (a fighter takes at most one hit from it), never above one boss hit (22%); no contact damage; launches go up or toward the middle, never off the world; nothing makes her unhittable; nothing is unavoidable; a run is never the answer on its own.

Six's hits are 0.9 of a boss hit (about 20%) unless noted. She never chases: a stand-off of 480 px (380 in phase 2) and a pop to wherever a move begins. ## 2. What the big stage does to Six, and what the designed five leave out

She is the stand-off boss, so the five are built to reach you: the bubbles home across any distance, the heap crosses the whole floor, the rocks are placed on each fighter, the slap and the kick are closed by a pop. That is enough to answer the owner's "you can just outrun them". What the five leave out, and the three new options each fill one gap:

1. Nothing in the five moves you. Every move waits for you to be somewhere; a fighter who hangs back near a wall is reached, but never made to come in. (ADDITION!, a pull.)
2. Nothing in the five has an answer other than "dodge her". There is no object you can attack to end a move, and no reason to step in towards her mid-move. (BUBBLE BLOWER 7000, a machine you can smash.)
3. The shield is only a health layer. No move of hers touches it, so the owner's signature (THE BUBBLE) is felt only between moves. (ROUND THE WORLD, the one move that uses it.)

Yardsticks used for every number: a run is 6.4 px a frame (read in the code; the slowest fighter 5.4 from a code comment on `FIGHTER_STATS`, the fastest 7.2 from the Four file); a full jump rises 126 px (computed from `JUMP` and `GRAV`; the weakest jumper 107 and a short hop 54, both from the Four file); a standing fighter is taken as about 100 px tall and 60 px wide (an assumption: the real hurtbox is a per-fighter ellipse); ledges and the centre platform are 144 px up (spec section 8). A lateral dodge must fit in the frames given at 5 px a frame; a jump needs a window of at least 9 frames at the weakest jump. Bands: medium 0.8 of a boss hit (17.6%, called 16 to 18%), heavy 0.9 to 1.0 (20 to 22%). If a stock holds about 240% (the Four file's assumption, not measured), a 20% hit is about 8% of one stock and about 2% of a four-stock run.

## 3. The three new options

All three: appear and grow in over 12 frames, hit once per turn, are placed relative to you at the lock (so distance never helps), leave her hittable throughout, and work with the leash (she starts within 650 px of you).

### 6. ADDITION!  [new; shape: a pull, area denial that moves you]
- Looks like: she plants both hands forward, palms open and lit light blue (the glow is at the source). Leaves and dust start streaming across the floor toward one spot, and a glowing plus sign (about 220 px wide and tall) grows out of the floor there in 12 frames, 400 px from the nearest fighter, on the side with more room and always inside 15% to 85% of the stage width (never against a wall); if you are running, 400 px ahead of the way you run. Wind-up 36 frames.
- Does: 24 frames after the plus sign stands, for 120 frames (2 s), every living fighter within 560 px of it (640 in phase 2) is dragged along the floor toward it: 5.0 px a frame at the core easing to 1.4 at the edge (phase 2: 5.8 down to 1.6); an airborne fighter feels half; ledges are no shelter (a fighter on one drifts off it). Then the plus sign flashes white for 8 frames (one flicker, no strobe) and snaps shut: anyone within 150 px of the core (190 in phase 2) is hit and thrown up and toward the middle. It folds away in 12 frames. No hit during the pull itself.
- Tell in the body: the lit open palms, the leaf stream (which also shows the direction of the pull), then the plus sign's own glow rising for the last 20 frames. Nothing on the floor but the sign.
- Dodge: walk or run out of the well (a run is 6.4 against a drag of at most 5.0, so every fighter, the slowest at 5.4 included, nets a way out; from 400 px the edge is 160 px away, 25 to 50 frames), or simply hold your ground anywhere beyond 150 px of the core at the flash. Do not run through it: the core is where the drag ends you, and a fighter who stands still at 400 px is carried in by about frame 100 and hit. In phase 2 the core out-drags the slowest runner, so do not be near it.
- Why distance stops working: it is placed on you, ahead of a runner, and the well grips 1,120 px of floor (1,280 in phase 2) wherever you are on a 2,400 px stage. It is the only move that makes a fighter who hangs back come in.
- Damage: heavy, 20% (0.9), one hit at the snap, knock 12 up and toward the middle.
- Canon: the 2,000,000 special: Six and Nine put both Sevens beside an addition symbol, it begins to glow, "Seven tries to flee, but the addition symbol forces both Sevens together" (read; the fleeing fails, which is this move). The 1,500,000 special: Six and Nine set a plus sign between them to make Fifteen (from the Six page). The Equation Playground page: the Playground has flowers shaped like plus, minus and asterisk (canon, "Alone"). The wiki marks the specials non-canon. The pull, the numbers and the snap are game text.
- Engine and size: about 60 lines. The drag is the loop already in `fourByeStep` (`f.x += dx/d*p`, easing with distance) and the Black Hole assist's drag; the sign is two rectangles and a glow drawn in code; a `_hz` bag holds its position and clock. Picker tag: `any` (it punishes standing still anywhere).
- Cousins: Four's GO BYE-BYE! (an inhale and a spit) is not in the owner's picks for Four, so this is the only pull in the rush. It is the third math sign in the rush (Zero's DIVIDE BY ZERO! chop, 2,763's Division Symbol refuges): a sign each, three different jobs.

### 7. BUBBLE BLOWER 7000  [new; shape: a placed turret with a vertical sweep, and a prop you can smash]
- Looks like: she reaches behind her and a toy bubble machine rolls out beside her (a pink-and-blue plastic barrel on two wheels with a funnel, drawn in code; grows in over 12 frames), the funnel pointing at the nearest fighter. She yanks its cord twice (two beats; the machine shudders and puffs two small harmless bubbles each time). Wind-up 44 frames.
- Does: for 200 frames the funnel nods up and down (from 2 to 38 degrees above flat, one nod every 80 frames; phase 2: every 64 frames, 3 more bubbles) while it keeps its side, spitting a big bubble (radius 34) every 14 frames at 6.5 px a frame, drifting up 0.12 a frame, life 120 frames (reach about 780 px). A bubble that touches a fighter hits and pops. The machine has 24 health (30 in phase 2): any fighter's hit takes a chunk, a hit or two smashes it, and when it breaks every bubble it made pops at once and the turn ends.
- Tell in the body: she pulls the machine out, the two cord yanks, the funnel's first nod. The machine's own shudder says "now".
- Dodge: at your distance the stream sweeps from ankle height (about 17 px up at 480 px) to chest and over your head; a low bubble (centre under 100 px, top under 94) is cleared by the weakest full jump (107 px), a high one passes over, so jump the low ones and stay down under the high ones, reading the nod. Or run in and smash the machine, which sits beside her: that ends the move and puts you next to her bubble.
- Why distance stops working: reach 780 px from a machine she places beside herself, and the sweep covers every height. The way out of the stream is to come in, not to run.
- Damage: medium, 16% (0.8 x 0.9 of 22), one hit per turn.
- Canon: "Alone": Six blows bubbles into Seven's eyes and winks at Ten, "The bubbles burn Seven's eyes" (checked); the Six page also says she opens X Finds Out His Value blowing bubbles. The Six page cites The Number Playground Chronicle, Section B: Six Integer "does not appear to be too enthusiastic about buying his Bubble Co. Baby Bubble Blower 7000" (a non-canon newspaper; the Chronicle's own page does not list it, so I could not check that line myself, and the machine is a prop, not an event). The machine, the sweep and the smash are game text.
- Engine and size: about 80 lines (the machine drawn in code 25, the sweep 25, the bubble shots reuse BUBBLES!'s shot 10, the smash and the pop-all 20). A prop with its own small health is new to the roster bosses, but the sentry and turret fighter kits already do it. Picker tag: `far`.
- Cousins: BUBBLES! (hers: from her mouth, small, homing, no machine). This one is a placed machine, a straight stream that does not home, a sweep up and down, and a prop to smash. If the owner wants her kit to stay varied, take at most one of BUBBLES! and this, or accept two bubble moves because bubbles are who she is. 2,763's LIGHTS is a spiral; this is a vertical sweep.

### 8. ROUND THE WORLD  [new; shape: a jump-in, the only move that uses her shield]
- Looks like: her bubble swells from 1.3x her body to about 1.6x over 24 frames and wobbles; she curls up inside it (pose `curl`: knees up, arms round them) with leaves swirling in the film. Wind-up 36 frames. Before every launch the bubble squashes flat on the floor for 12 frames into a disc about 340 px wide with a puff of leaves: the squash is the tell for the next arc.
- Does: three big arcs, each 520 to 760 px long, 64 frames in the air at 9 to 12 px a frame, centre 330 px up at the top; each lands where the nearest fighter stood when the squash began (phase 2: four arcs of 58 frames, the third and fourth led by 14 frames of that fighter's run). It hurts while an arc is live and during the 12-frame squash; no shock waves (the leaves are a picture). After the last landing it wobbles 24 frames, harmless and hittable (no damage bonus, no stun), then she pops back to her stand-off.
- The shield: while her bubble stands, the rolling ball is the bubble (radius about 135); your hits go into its pool as always, and if the pool hits zero in mid-roll the bubble pops, she falls where she is, dazed 90 frames, and the move is over: the daze window you earned, taken from the middle of her best move. Once the bubble is gone (phase 1 after the pop), she tucks into her plain body (radius 85) and rolls the same arcs, a little smaller.
- Tell in the body: the swell, the curl, then the squash before each launch. No line, no ring.
- Dodge: step 200 px out of the landing (31 frames of the 76 you have), or walk about 150 px toward her to stand under the middle of the arc: the bubble's underside there is 195 px up, well over a standing head (the weakest short hop is cleared too), so about the middle 380 px of each arc is safe floor. Never jump under the top: a full jump's head reaches 226 px. A ledge is not safe: the arc passes through ledge height (144 to 244 px) mid-flight, so a fighter on a ledge should drop. Or hit the bubble.
- Why distance stops working: three arcs cover about 1,800 px a turn (up to 2,300 in phase 2), faster than any run, each landing on you; she crosses the room and is never left behind.
- Damage: medium, 18% (0.8), once per turn, knock 10 and 8 up toward the middle.
- Canon: the 700,000 special: Seven kicks Six out of the subscriber count and she spins around the globe, screaming; the 800,000 special: Four snatches her out of the sky (both read). The Six page abilities: flight and levitation, forceful propulsion, barrier binding (the Moon seal in "Alone"). The bubble ball is game text, because the bubble shield is game text.
- Engine and size: about 70 lines. The arc is a short script like Two's pop and hop; the state is the existing `_hz.bb` (pool, max, popped-at) plus the arc's clock; a pop during the roll ends the script and starts the daze. Picker tag: `far`.
- Cousins, honestly: it is the fourth bouncing ball in the rush's options: Two's SUN HUNT (a spiked sun hops 6 to 8 times at 14 px a frame, re-aimed each hop, ends after one hit), Three's THE CATCH (a tossed ball with an arch and a return), Zero's GET HIM! (a rolling cloud that bounces off a wall). What is different: it is the boss herself inside her own shield; three arcs only and no re-aim in the air; slower; the pop-in-mid-roll rule; ledges are hit. If the owner does not want another ball, the options to leave it out for are the five designed ones plus the other two new ones (the spares in section 5 were each dropped for a reason).

## 5. Notes

- THE SIGNATURE: THE BUBBLE (the shield that soaks the first 120 damage of each phase, pops into a daze window, and re-forms at 50%) is not an attack: it is the engine's hit pipeline, so it stays whatever five, seven or eight moves are picked. The attack that carries her identity and must stay is BUBBLES! (her signature move, from her first scene, the same material as the shield, and her only reach into the air). ROUND THE WORLD is the only option that touches the shield; if the owner wants the shield to matter inside her moves, that is the pick, but nothing breaks without it.
- Kit size and pace: gaps are unchanged (170 / 130 base, times the engine's 1.2), so if the owner picks seven or eight each move shows up less often; the picker never draws the same one twice in a row. The spec budgeted about 830 lines for the five; the new ones add roughly 60 (ADDITION!), 80 (BUBBLE BLOWER 7000) and 70 (ROUND THE WORLD). Each is a pure function of her `_hz` bag and the clock, as the house habit asks.
- Suggested sets, if the owner wants one: six or seven = the five designed plus ADDITION! (the only move that moves you) and BUBBLE BLOWER 7000 (the only one you smash); add ROUND THE WORLD for the shield. If the owner wants only five, the cheapest to drop is SLAP! (its closing job is KICK OUT!'s, and X's ARROW DASH! and Three's HALLWAY LASER already take rows). These are suggestions; the owner chooses.
- Spare canon, not counted (designed, then left out, with the reason):
  - JENGA!: the best canon gag on the Six page (the 2,000,000 special: she tugs a block, the tower wobbles, she stops, tugs another, it wobbles). A tower that sways three times and tips over you. Dropped: Nine's THE STACK! is the same three-beat sway and tip, and Two's TOWER ROW is toppling stacks.
  - DIG SIDEWAYS!: her point on top as a shark fin racing at you under the floor. Dropped: one more ground chase (Two's SUN HUNT, Four's CACTUS DRIVE, X's TAG, 2,763's ROOT THEM OUT, Zero's GET HIM! and Nine's SKATE AWAY! already are), and burrowing makes her unhittable unless the fin is made a hurtbox.
  - TRIPPED!: a leg stretched across the floor ahead of a runner, sprung when crossed ("Alone": she trips Ten as he runs around nearby). Dropped: a low bar is trivial to hop, and X's LEG BOOMERANG! is a leg already.
  - SWING SET, SEESAW (the seesaw is canon scenery in "Alone", Four and X asleep on it), SIX'S CAT (a black cat with white face markings, a trivia fact only): each tested on paper and each either a static no-go zone, a lob in disguise, or too small to read on 2,400 px.
  - A PLAYGROUND BULLY! string (trip, slap, kick, all three canon acts): its beats copy SLAP! and KICK OUT! and cannot all be dodged by one input at a fair spacing.
