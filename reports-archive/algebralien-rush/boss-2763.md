# Boss 9 of 9: 2763 (Two Thousand Seven Hundred Sixty-Three). Canon check and boss design

Prepared 2026-11-02 for the Algebralien Boss Rush design pass (lens: "the boss 2763"). Read-only study of `smash-bal` (branch ii-dlc, live main). Nothing in the game was edited.

Labels used below: **CANON** = read on the wiki (the page is named). **GAME** = a decision this design makes, never canon. **PROPOSAL** = an idea for the owner to accept or refuse. Game text is marked as game text.

---

## 0. The short version

- **What 2763 is.** A real character, found on the BFDI wiki: an Algebralien made of the number 2,763, the main antagonist of two *subscriber specials* (Feb 2025 and Sep 2026). It is an omnipotent-looking giant that traps the other Algebraliens in a pocket void and floods the world with lava. It is **not** a TPOT episode character: the wiki calls the specials non-canon to the main BFDI continuity. Nothing about it needs inventing.
- **The fight.** A hovering, code-drawn giant of four glowing numerals with six black arms and ten eyes, fought in "The Void" over a rising lava floor. Its signature is THE DRAIN: near it you cannot use specials or finishers (it drains your power), and what it drains becomes the lights it throws at you. Five attacks: SLAM, WINDMILL, LIGHTS, CAGE, FLOOD. Two phases (three HP bands in the engine). It ends the way canon ends it: the giant Zero stomps it.
- **Size: large** (about 1,100 lines, range 900 to 1,350; medium if CAGE is cut). **No new image art** (code-drawn body). One new arena key.
- **Three things the owner must see first** (details in section 9): (1) at the owner's 3,000 hp the fight is about 6 to 7 minutes long, so I lowered hits per minute and propose three real acts; (2) the rush's stages are larger, so the design asks the stage for little (a flat floor, a camera, the leash), brings its own refuge (the Division Symbol), and from phase 2 builds its own lava island so nobody can outrun it; (3) the drain silences recovery specials too.

### 1.2 The character sheet (CANON, BFDI wiki)

- **Species and looks.** An Algebralien that is an integer: "Two Thousand Seven Hundred Sixty-Three Integer". Four numerals, each its own colour: the 2 is rose (Macaroon Rose #F1527F), the 7 cyan (Neon Nazar #50E1E4), the 6 lavender (#B771E7), the 3 yellow (Summertime #F4D37B). **Limbs are near-black** (Viola Black #2F2B3E) with a black outline, like the Objects' and Seven's. It has **10 eyes** (three on the 2 and the 7, two on the 6 and the 3), **6 mouths**, **5 legs** and **6 arms** (two on the 2, one on the 7, three on the 3), but it can change those counts at will. First four-digit Algebralien, longest name of any. Pronouns: it/its in the first special; the voice actor later wrote "he".
- **Voice.** Jackie Skinner.
- **Personality.** Omnipresent, calm, rational, intimidating. It does not want to kill the others, it wants to **trap** them (jail, a pocket void); its stated end is destroying the world. It polite-monologues, greets its prisoners, is irritated by interruptions, and dislikes Ten's music. Nicknames: That Thing, Mister Mysterious Thing, The Most Powerful Number.
- **Powers (all from its page and the tape in the first special).** Strongest of all Algebraliens as claimed (immeasurable power, listed as formerly omnipotent); **lava manipulation** (raises and floods lava at will); **power draining** (merely being near other Algebraliens drains their strength, powers and stamina); absorbs the powers of the other numbers; **super strength** (lifts a whole metal cage; its tape calls the arms herculean); **heat vision** (sees heat through walls); **telepathy** (reads thoughts, projects its own voice into your mind); **extra limbs at will**; **size manipulation** (stands thousands of feet tall, can grow); **voice projection across dimensions**. It also fires a charged **laser** at the giant Zero in the second special.
- **Weakness.** Ten's guitar (a sticker on it says it kills omnipotent evil numbers). And **Zero**: its powers only work on numbers, and Zero is nothing. In the second special Ten types 2763 divided by 0 into a calculator and the calculator vanishes; that is the plan Ten and Zero set out on, which is why the Book of Division and the division symbol matter (Fourteen later smashes the symbol).
- **How it acts.** Part 1: unseen voice and lava rising to flush the others out; a cage it carries into the air with prisoners inside; huge black hands crash through a window and take Two, Five, Six, Seven and Eight, deliberately skipping Zero. Part 2: from its **void** it counts its prisoners, brings three more (Four, X, and Five Hundred Sixty-Two), charges a beam at the giant Zero, and its powers do nothing to him.
- **How it ends.** Stomped by the giant fused Zero (listed as a death on both pages, marked debatable). Right after, its friend Three Thousand Five Hundred Seventeen (red, bigger, and by its own boast worse; its page lists the two as allies) appears and is stomped too.
- **The one quote I will use.** To Zero in the first special: "you're nothing" (spoken by 2763; the first-special page).
- **Place.** Its void: a large area made after part 1, landmark **the Division Symbol**, the inhabitants (formerly) Two, Four, X, Five, Six, Seven, Eight, Nine, Ten and others. Part 1 is the Math Planet and Zero's bunker flooding with lava.

## 3. Position, HP and how long the fight is

**Position:** 9 of 9. Owner's curve: Two 1,000, Four 1,200, Nine 1,400, Six 1,600, Zero 1,800, X 2,100, One 2,400, Three 2,700, **2763 3,000**.

**Expected length (an estimate, from the game's own harness data, not a test I ran):** `scripts/boss-rush-balance.json` records how long mid-pack fighters take to clear a boss and its hp. Dividing hp by clear time gives 6.5 to 9 hp per second for the seven bosses listed (Four: 510 hp in 63.7 s is 8.0). The same fighters' rate against a 3,000 bar is **375 to 460 s, about 6 to 7.5 minutes**, and the drain lowers the rate a little because specials are the damage a lot of kits lean on. The fit pass (`fit.md`, section 3.2) uses 6, 8 and 10 hp per second and gets **8:20, 6:15 and 5:00 for 2763**, which agrees; I take **5 to 8 minutes, about 6 at the middle** (a larger stage costs damage time, so lean slow). If the x1.5 also applies it is 1.5 times that. The whole run at 17,200 total hp is about 30 to 48 minutes of fighting (about 36 at the middle), plus intros and endings. The knob `fit.md` proposes (`ALG_RUSH.dmgToBoss`, one number read where a boss takes damage) shortens every fight by one factor and keeps the bars reading 1k to 3k; this design needs nothing else from it. Say it once to the owner; it is a long run.

**What that means for the design (GAME):**
1. The fight is about six times longer than the game's usual boss. If its hits per minute were Four's, a player would lose more stocks than the rush gives (start 4, +2 per boss, max 6). So 2763's gaps are longer than Four's (120 / 96 / 70 frames before the engine's x1.2 pace, Four's are 104 / 78 / 56) and its hits are 0.6 to 0.9 of a boss hit instead of 0.8 to 1.0. The target is a good human losing **about 3 of 6 stocks**, not zero and not six. This is not tuning to a bot; it is the stock rule applied to a long fight.
2. Three HP bands is one band every 1,000 hp, about 2 minutes each. One kit for 2 minutes is a lot; so the second phase has a real change in each of its two bands (section 7).
3. With about 75 turns in a whole fight, five attacks repeat about 15 times each, softened by the variants (2 / 4 / 6 hands, 3 / 6 arms, 1 / 2 spirals, 1 / 2 cages, 1 / 2 tides). I list two optional extra moves in section 10 if the owner finds it thin.

## 4. Arena: The Void (a new key, `void2763`)

**Canon fit.** Its pocket void, with the Division Symbol as landmark; part 1's flooded Math Planet gives the lava. The older `void` key (a dark sky, "algebralian void") exists in the sky table with nothing using it; I recommend a new key so this does not collide with whatever the other eight bosses pick.

**The stage it needs.** The owner wants the rush's stages larger, and `fit.md` (section 3.8) makes **each boss its own match**, so 2763 can have its own stage. I do not know its size. Every pixel number in this file is in the game's 1,100 x 720 view (the camera size), which stays true on a bigger stage, and the design asks the stage for only three things: a flat solid floor, a camera that frames the boss and the fighters, and the leash (the boss is never farther than about half a view from them). Nothing here depends on the stage having a platform.

**Look.**
- Reference numbers, today's boss arena: world 1,100 x 720, floor line y = 590, floor x 110 to 990 with a cliff at both ends, one wide platform at y 446.
- The sky is a deep violet to dull ember (not pure black: its limbs are near-black and must read). The ground is dark violet with glowing orange cracks (a `BOSS_ARENA_GROUND` pattern: glow in the cracks, stronger in later phases). Decor: slow drifting numerals and symbols (a few plus, minus, times, equals signs), no text. It is roofed (no clouds): add the key to `BOSS_ARENA_ROOFED`.
- **Lava light.** Everything near-black is lit from below by the lava (a thin orange rim on the lower edge of each arm), so the limbs show against the violet.

**The Division Symbol is FLOOD's own refuge, not a stage platform (canon: it is the landmark of its void).** When the tide rises, one or more Division Symbols rise out of the floor under the middle of the tide's span: a solid bar 300 wide, 110 px over the floor, with a round dot above and one hanging below, in the same black outline. One symbol per two fighters (1 to 3). They sink when the tide ebbs. This way FLOOD works on any stage; a stage's own platforms and any standing cage are extra refuges. (On today's boss arena the stage's wide platform, 144 px up, does the same job; both work.)

**Hazard, phase 2 on: THE ISLAND.** The first tide of phase 2 does not drain all the way: the floor stays lava everywhere except **a dry island centred where the boss stands** when it ebbs, **720 px wide** in band 2 and **560 px** in band 3 (the island shrinks at the band change, growing in over 12 frames the other way: the lava creeping in). From then on the boss is held to the island and the camera frames it, so on any stage the second half of the fight is one readable screen and **nobody can outrun it** (the owner's complaint about Two and Four). On today's 1,100-wide arena the island's edges are exactly the 80 px lava ends (x 110 to 190, 910 to 990) and then 160 px. Standing in lava: 0.25 of a boss hit every 40 frames, a small hop up, no knockback away (never a trap, since the hop clears it). One interval, one rule. The tide's wind-up and the lights' shot life scale with the stage: wind-up 72 plus 1 frame per 20 px of the tide's span over 1,100; shot life `ceil(span / speed) + 8` (as Springy's shots do).

## 5. The body: code-drawn, and what art exists

**Art check** (`artifacts/V1/assets/sprites`): Two has `two.png`, Four has nine files (`four.png` plus screech, bye-bye, zap, tower, love, puddle, mad, next-level-mad), One has `one.png` and `one-fish.png`. There is **nothing for 2763**, Nine, Six, Zero, X or Three. The wiki has art for 2763, none of it in the game: `Gud lookin render of 2763.png` (1739 x 992, the infobox render), `2763Character.png` (3546 x 1694, no background), `Cleanedasset.png` (1913 x 778), `Theflatof2763.png` (1280 x 720). Every other boss in the game wears a wiki render, taken through `scripts/fetch-sprites.mjs` and credited in `CREDITS.md`.

**Recommendation (GAME): draw it in code, ship no image.** Reasons: its identity is limbs and eyes at will and resizing, and a flat render cannot sprout arms on a count the player reads. A code body can (the engine's `body` slot is exactly this: the Bug Swarm has no render at all). The tube numerals are about 60 lines of stroked paths (not a font, so it cannot fail to load, and a client or a headless boot draws the same). It also needs no `CREDITS.md` entry. The wiki render stays an option for the event card or a thumbnail only.

**The drawing.** Four tube numerals in a row about 235 px wide and 165 tall, each a black stroke with the numeral's colour inside and a soft inner glow; eyes: 10 white ovals with a black pupil that looks at the nearest fighter (the telepathy: it sees you); mouths: 6, closed lines that open on certain tells; 5 short black legs hanging; arms: black tubes with three-finger hands; the arms **grow out of the numerals in canon places** (2: two, 7: one, 3: three) and grow in over 12 frames.

**Movement (GAME).** Hovering, belly 60 px over the floor (Four's float is 20 to 64), a slow bob, no chase: in the first phase it **glides toward the fighters' centre** (0.6 px a frame, faster only when the leash needs it) so a larger stage does not turn it into a statue or let a player walk off from it; from the island on (phase 2) it is held to the island; it drops 30 px for the second phase (it peers down). The roster row says `stationary:true` with its own move function, as MePhone4's does, so the engine's default walk is skipped. No contact damage anywhere on its body (nothing in the game's giant bosses needs it, and the arms and lights do the work).

## 6. Attacks

All in wind-up units; the engine runs a wind-up `BOSS_TEL_PACE` times longer (I propose **1.25**, Four's), so the real time is 1.25x the number. Damage is a share of one boss hit (`bossDmg()`), and a turn has one attack id, so a fighter takes **at most one hit from one turn**, as every overhauled boss does. Every tell is in the body or in the world, never a line, ring, banner or reticle. **A falling thing is live while it falls.** Anything that appears grows in over 12 frames.

| # | Attack (name for code and this file only) | Reads like | Tags (the picker) | Wind-up | Damage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SLAM** | hands from above | ground, platform | 54 | 0.9 |
| 2 | **WINDMILL** | six arms turning round it | close | 48 | 0.8 |
| 3 | **LIGHTS** | the lights it stole, thrown in a spiral | far, air | 50 | 0.6 each |
| 4 | **CAGE** | the cage it carries, dropped on whoever is farthest | far, wall | 60 | 0.9 |
| 5 | **FLOOD** (second phase on) | the lava rises over the floor | ground | 72 | 0.7, then 0.2 |

Gaps between turns (before the engine's pace): **120 / 96 / 70** by HP band. The first attack comes 150 frames after the intro ends.

**1. SLAM (arms of the 2 and the 3).** *Tell (body):* on a two-count it rises, glowing rose, and N black arms sprout from its top (grow in over 12 frames each, 8 frames apart), hands open, one over each target. The hands follow their fighter, then **lock for the last 16 frames**. A real shadow grows on the floor under each hand over its last 36 frames (the only warning in the world). *Fire:* each hand falls over 14 frames (live the whole way down), hits 0.9, knockback away from the hand and up, `impact()` shake and a floor scar; the hand sits 40 frames, then withdraws. Hands per fighter: one on each fighter (up to the cap), the cap is **2 hands in the first band, 4 in the second, 6 in the third** (all six arms). Fighters on the platform get theirs on the platform. *Counter:* leave the shadow after the lock. *Unfair spot to guard:* two hands on one fighter: the second goes to the next fighter, or to a spot ahead of the lone fighter's run (never the same spot).

**2. WINDMILL (the six arms).** *Tell:* it coils on a three-count, glowing lavender, and arms grow out of it around the compass (3 in the first band, 6 later), then start turning slowly: that **slow turn is the warning**; the body shudders; it speeds up over the last 12 frames of the wind-up. *Fire:* the arms turn one revolution (first band, 140 frames at 0.045 rad a frame, 3 arms) or 1.5 revolutions (6 arms, 0.04 rad a frame), arm length 240 px, thickness about 28. A hit is 0.8 with knockback outward and up. The arms do not draw below the floor. *Counter:* step outside 240 px, or jump an arm (it is thin) as it passes. The picker uses it when someone is close, which is where the drain also is, so the circle you are drained in is nearly the circle that is struck. *Ends:* the arms fold back into the numerals (12 frames).

**3. LIGHTS (the stolen power).** *Tell:* the lights that orbit its body speed up and brighten, glowing cyan, and lift off one at a time on the beats (see the drain). *Fire:* N orbs leave in a spiral, one every 7 frames, each rotated 0.62 rad from the last (a spiral of up to 285 degrees), speed 4.4, radius 12, 0.6 each. **N is however many lights it is carrying (3 to 8), then it drops back to 3.** Second band: a second spiral, **opposite way**, starts 28 frames after the first. Third band: the orbs go 5.2 fast and N gets +2. Each orb wears the colour of one beaten boss, in the order they were beaten (read from the rush rows: Two, Four, Nine, Six, Zero, X, One, Three), so by the ninth boss the player has seen every colour. *Counter:* weave, spend a jump, or stand where the spiral is thin; the further you are, the more time you have.

**4. CAGE (the trap, GAME: the canon cage).** *Tell:* one arm lifts a barred cage above its head and rattles it on two beats, glowing rose and yellow; a real shadow grows on the floor under the **farthest fighter in view** over the last 40 frames and locks for the last 16 (a move for whoever keeps away; it never targets someone beyond the leash). *Fire:* the cage falls over 14 frames (live while it falls): hit 0.9, knockback out from the cage's centre line and up, so nobody is ever caught inside; it **stays** 220 frames as a solid box (about 110 wide, 130 tall, bars drawn), standing as a wall and a place to stand on top, then an arm lifts it away over 20 frames. Second band: two cages, 40 frames apart. If only one fighter is alive, the second cage lands at the mirror of the first about the boss. *Risks:* it is the same family as Four's hills and Two's block towers; see section 10.

**5. FLOOD (lava, canon: it raises and floods lava; second phase on).** *Tell (world + body):* embers and bubbling at both ends of the tide's span (a span about 1.3 views wide, centred on the fighters) over the first 36 frames; then the floor's cracks glow from both ends toward the middle over the last 36 frames; the boss lowers all its arms toward the floor. *Fire:* over 12 frames the lava rises to **56 px** on the floor and hits anyone standing on it (the rise is live while rising, 0.7), holds 70 frames (0.2 every 36 frames to anyone in it), then ebbs over 20 frames. Third band: a second surge to **84 px** after 30 frames. *Refuge:* the Division Symbols that rise with it (section 4), any cage standing, any platform the stage has. *Counter:* go up. The wind-up is **72 plus 1 frame per 20 px of the span past 1,100**, so a larger stage gives the extra run time. The first tide is also the **opening of the second phase** (the picker's `first`), and its ebb is what leaves the island; nobody meets it cold.

Optional tell-by-colour: each attack's glow is one numeral's colour, so players learn rose = hands or cage, lavender = windmill, cyan = lights, yellow = flood, with the pose (rise, coil, puff, crouch) as the shape. This is the engine's own `BOSS_TELLS` (pose, glow, beats).

**Picker tags and gates (BOSS_PICK):** SLAM `['ground','platform']`; WINDMILL `'close'`; LIGHTS `['far','air']`; CAGE `['far','wall']`; FLOOD `'ground'`. First band moves: SLAM, WINDMILL, LIGHTS, CAGE. Second phase adds FLOOD (`first`), and its busy test covers tide, cages, arms, hands and the spiral.

## 7. Signature mechanic: THE DRAIN

**Canon:** by being near others it drains their power; it absorbs powers; the tape says so.

**Rule (GAME).**
1. **Inside 220 px of its body centre** (about 2.15 x its radius, a little less than the windmill's reach), fighters are **hushed**: no specials and no finishers (a Final Smash still goes). This reuses the game's own hush status (`_hushT`, set a little ahead of `hazardT` every frame, so it also wears off about 20 frames after you leave), which is already drawn as a status glyph and already in the netcode snapshot. The boss itself is not hushed.
2. **What it drains becomes lights.** The lights orbiting its body are its gauge: it starts with 3, gains one for every 45 frames that any fighter spends inside the aura (to 8), and LIGHTS throws them all. So standing close gives you melee range and costs you your specials and thickens its spiral; staying back keeps your specials and thins the spiral.
3. **No ring is drawn.** The aura's edge is shown by motes of light that stream from drained fighters into the numerals (they only start inside the reach), and by the status glyph on the fighter. No outline or circle.

**Why this one:** no other boss changes what the fighters can do; it is cheap (about 70 lines), canon, readable (a growing halo of coloured lights), and it fits a long fight because it changes decisions rather than adding damage.

**Needs the owner's yes** (it silences recovery specials as well; see section 9, risk 3). Alternative if refused: **slows** instead (the game has a `slowed` status from the quicksand), same motes, same gauge.

## 8. Intro, defeat, how it should look

**Intro (about 170 frames, no text but one sign).**

**Defeat (GAME, but the canon exit; no text; holds about 3,000 ms, the way Four's ending holds the rush victory card).** The arms go limp and fall away over 12 frames; a **real shadow grows on it from above for 40 frames**, and the eight lights rise out of it and hang in a ring; then **a giant grey foot, the giant Zero's, comes down** (code-drawn, in the Zero boss's own roster colour; `boss-zero.md` already builds a Giant Zero and THE FOOT, so this reuses that drawer if it exists, or about 40 lines of its own if not): an `impact()` shake and dust, the body squashes flat in 6 frames, the four numerals pop out and tumble, then fade over 40 frames; the eight lights fly away in eight directions (the powers go home), and the lava drains and the Void's floor goes quiet. Then the nine-boss victory card. **Optional sting (the owner's call):** the last 40 frames, two red eyes open far off in the dark and close (3,517, its friend, who turns up right after it falls in the second special). No text.

**How it looks cool.** Four neon numerals against a violet void with ember light from below; black limbs rim-lit orange; ten eyes that look at you and blink out of step; a halo of coloured lights that thickens when you stand near; the Division Symbols rising out of the lava as your refuge; sky and floor going from violet to ember red across the fight; hands, cages and the foot all arriving with real shadows and screen shake. The first thing the player sees is eyes in the dark, which is the canon reveal, and it is cheap to draw.

**House-rule check.** No telegraph lines or warning lines, no rings, no reticles: tells are the body (digits glow, arms sprout, spin, coil), shadows under what falls, embers and glowing cracks at the source. Everything growing in over 12 frames: arms, eyes, lights, hands, cages, lava, Division Symbols, the sign, the island's lava creeping in. Falling traps hit while falling: SLAM, CAGE and the tide's rise. Computer only (nothing touch). ## 9. What could go wrong

1. **Fight length and stocks (largest).** At 3,000 hp it is 5 to 8 minutes. If the hits per minute are the game's usual, stocks run out (start 4, max 6). I lowered the pace and the damage shares (section 3). The owner should confirm the target of "about 3 of 6 stocks lost" or ask for easier.
2. **The larger stage.** I do not know the rush stage's size. Everything is in the game's 1,100 x 720 view numbers, and the design asks the stage only for a flat floor, a camera that frames the boss and the fighters, and the leash (section 4). SLAM (on each fighter), LIGHTS (all round), CAGE (on the farthest in view) and FLOOD (over a span round the fighters) cannot be outrun; WINDMILL can, and is only picked when someone is close; and from phase 2 the island holds everyone in. The owner said Two and Four need a rush version because they can be outrun; for 2763 the answer is the leash in the first half and the island in the second. **Risk:** the island needs the camera to lock on it (the stage work: `fit.md` risk 2 says the game keeps boss fights on one screen because a panning camera makes tells unreadable).
3. **The drain also silences recovery specials.** The hush blocks the special key in every direction. The aura sits round the boss (reach 220), and on today's arena that is the middle (x 330 to 770), away from the cliffs, but a fighter launched up and out through the aura could be silenced in the air and lose an up-special recovery. Options: hush only fighters near the floor or the platform; or give a recovery exemption; or use the slow alternative. The owner's call.
4. **The tide and reaching the refuge.** The Division Symbol rises 110 px over the floor (the stage platform, if used, is 144). A single jump is about 126 px (`JUMP -12.5`, gravity 0.62); fighters get two jumps (`f.jumps = 2` on landing), so 110 px should always be reachable, but a **heavy fighter with a short jump** is the one to check on a real kit. The FLOOD must not start while a fighter is down in a recovery or respawning (the picker's busy test), and the wind-up lengthens with the span. A fighter standing on a symbol when it sinks drops onto a floor that is dry by then (it ebbs first).
5. **The island's edge and the cliff.** A fighter recovering from a cliff, or knocked off the island, may land in lava (hit 0.25, small hop up, no knockback) rather than on safe floor. The hop up is intentional so it is never a trap; the island is never smaller than 560 px.
6. **CPU allies.** The CPU code does not read the hush (it keeps pressing specials inside the aura: wasted presses, not a crash) and I could not find where it reads the tide as danger. Verify allies leave the floor for a tide the way they leave a coaster mark; if not, register the tide wherever the CPU reads boss danger.
7. **Online.** The boss's fields must be numbers in the snapshot (`net` slot and `netProjectiles`): `lit` (which numeral is glowing), arms out and their angles and lengths, windmill angle, the lights count, the tide level, a list of cages (x, age), hands (x, age), intro and ending clocks. All drawing is from those numbers and `hazardT`, as Four's hazard does. The cage walls and the tide are the physics that a client must also have (the way Four's hills are solid `worldPlats`): check that a guest standing on a cage does not snap back.
8. **Stuck spots.** A fighter between a cage and the island's lava; a fighter inside the boss's circle (no contact damage, so it is only a bad place, not a stuck one). Cages always throw the target out, so nobody lands inside one. Cages must not land on a Division Symbol while the tide is up (they would stack): skip a cage's target if it stands on one.
9. **The engine's roster gates.** `bossPaced` (the picker, gaps and telegraph pace) is true only for rows of `BOSS_ROSTER`; the loop ends on the boss named Four (`wasFour`); `awardBossCleared` only knows `BOSS_ROSTER` names. The event's own nine-row list needs `bossPaced` to see it, an end test on the last row, and its names in the profile list. This is shared by all nine bosses and belongs to the lead, once.
10. **Flash.** The intro flash is a soft 12-frame rise and fall, never a strobe (the code comments say nothing in the arenas flashes).
