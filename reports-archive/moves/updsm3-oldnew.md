## How to read the table

- "Old" is the generated pair: up = "a wide swing over your head that launches them upward" (reach 92 px), down = "a low sweep that hits both sides and sends them low and out" (reach 106 px). Only the name and the numbers differed between fighters.
- Numbers are damage / launch strength, per hit, before the fighter's own damage setting (Tree's is 1.04, the others 1).
- **Every new hit is at or under the old one in damage and in launch strength, and the most one foe can take from the whole move is at or under the old damage.** No sweet spot goes above the old move: the sweet spot is the old strength (or a little under), the sour part is well under it. So the hooks cost power, they do not add it. Three hooks are not numbers and could still make a move better than its numbers say: they are listed under "Where a hook could make a move stronger".
- Each move still starts with the smash charge (the ring, 24 to 32 frames), which is its wind-up: nothing can hurt before it. No move is shorter in frames than the one it replaces; Lollipop's down smash is 8 frames longer.
- Nothing falls from the sky in any of the 24.
- **I could not check any episode. Every "from the show" below is from memory.** Where I am not sure I say so. Please correct me.
- **I have not SEEN any of the 24 drawings** (no browser was allowed). They run without errors; how they look is unchecked.

## Where a hook could make a move stronger than its numbers (so you can judge)

The numbers above are all at or under the old move. These are the things numbers do not show:

1. **David's "No!" has 10 frames of armour.** That is new strength. It is paid for with damage (23.8 -> 20) and launch (12.2 -> 11.4). It does not cover the charge, only the first 10 frames after it.
2. **Bell's up smash throws sideways.** A sideways throw is more dangerous near an edge than an upward one of the same strength. I set its strength to 5.9, just under his OLD down smash (5.92), which also threw sideways, so he could already do this from the ground; now he can do it to a foe above him. In my ledge test a still dummy over him lost the stock sooner than with the old move (frames 46 to 51 against 55 to 72), but it lost it with the old move too, so the test proves little.
3. **Gaty's Return to Sender can throw diagonally** (never flatter than 45 degrees). At the steepest it is as strong sideways as about 0.7 of her old up smash, which is under her old down smash.
4. **David's shove and Nickel's field throw straight away from the fighter**, so a foe off to the side goes out diagonally. Both are under the old DOWN smash's strength (David 11.4 against 11.41, Nickel 11.5 against 12.68).
5. **Barf Bag's Turbulence throws down.** On the floor that only puts the foe back on the floor. Off a ledge a downward throw is a spike. I made it weak (4.3, under half the old launch) and turned it a little toward her for that reason. My ledge test could not tell old from new: a dummy that does not move, hung just past the edge, lost the stock with the OLD move too, at 0%, 30% and 60% (old: frames 44, 46, 32; new, with the stronger slam I started with: 36, 34, 32). With the weaker slam of the final build the same dummy at 60% lost the stock on frame 38. So **this is not proven safe**: the test cannot show whether a foe who fights back would get home.
6. **Tree's Bottled, Donut's bury and Donut's bolt stun** hold a foe for up to half a second. Tree and Donut are both still busy when the foe gets free (measured), but a team-mate in Teams gets a free hit.
7. **Taco heals 3%** per hit of her up smash. Paid for with damage (15 -> 12.5).
