Branch g-nsv2 (worktree .worktrees/nsv2). Not pushed, not merged. No relay touched. Scripts and raw data: nsv2-scripts/.
Damage is shown after the x1.5 multiplier, per hit, on a dummy at 0 percent. Launch is the length of the knockback vector.

## Checks and honest results

1. Per-hit damage and launch, old vs new (probe.mjs, 11 foe places each): none above the old. No errors, no hang in the probe. The probe's own "tail percent" line is unreliable (the dummy is launched and respawns) and was not used.
2. No-op proof (hash.mjs, 4 scenarios x 1500 frames, every fighter state hashed each frame): old build and new build with the 7 old rows put back are IDENTICAL on all 4 (b79799f3, d468382d, 65a41b0a, 3373d29f). The new build as it is differs only in the two scenarios that contain the 7 fighters. NOTE: the first version of hash.mjs was not deterministic (the OLD build gave different hashes on two identical runs; performance.now budgets). I added a fake deterministic clock to the harness for both builds; with it the old build repeats exactly. That is the only change to the harness. Limit: the two scenarios without the 7 fighters prove little by themselves; the oldrows match on the other two is the real proof for the shared code.
3. syncheck failed 0; bootcheck clean (no errors, 0 control bytes, 0 bare LF, no hidden names). Run again after the two number changes of step 8: still clean.
4. Look-alike (lookalike.mjs, shape + effect + numbers against every neutral row): no other row has the same shape as Marshmallow, Bonesaw, Tissues, Goo or Apple. Firey Jr. shares the "through" shape with 5 rows (weaken, knockdown, scramble, stun, slow) and none burns. Tapey shares "mine" with 10 rows (bleed, poison, root, ice, freeze, slow, burn, knockdown) and none stuns. No row has the same shape AND effect, none within 3 of the same numbers. The comparison is by shape name and numbers, not by pixels. The generated up/down smashes are one shared generic shape for every fighter, so only the numbers differ (listed above).
5. CPU matches (cpu.mjs, 1v1 against a panel of 12 ordinary fighters, 2 stocks, up to 3 minutes, 32 per fighter per build): 0 errors in all 14 runs. The move came out in every one (smash uses over 32 matches, old to new): Marshmallow 38 to 38, Bonesaw 22 to 37, Tissues 79 to 33, Firey Jr. 22 to 29, Goo 58 to 56, Apple 23 to 34, Tapey 102 to 41. Stuck CPU: Goo shows a stand-still of 120 frames or more in 14 of 32 new matches and 18 of 32 old ones, so it is not new; nobody else has one. Tissues and Tapey are used about 2.4 times LESS by the CPU than before (the shorter reach, 190 and a trap, means the CPU's range check fires less). These runs were made at the spec numbers, before the two number changes of step 8 (Tissues 10.5, Tapey stun 39); they were not repeated.
   Two of the first runs (Marshmallow and Goo, new build) came back empty because their processes were killed (or their launcher exited), not because of a hang: re-run in full, they finished with 0 errors; a separate trace of Marshmallow also finished every match.
6. Win share, wins of 32 (same seeds both builds, but the matches diverge once anything differs):
   Marshmallow 5 to 2, Bonesaw 25 to 21, Tissues 7 to 2, Firey Jr. 20 to 23, Goo 5 to 7, Apple 13 to 21, Tapey 18 to 13.
   With 32 matches the noise is about 4 matches either way (the balance notes put an unpaired swing at 7.5 points median). Beyond noise: Apple +8 (up 25 points), about two sigma, flag it. Tissues -5 and Marshmallow -3 are falls from already low numbers, borderline. Nothing else is clearly beyond noise. I did not change anything for these; one batch of 32 cannot separate them. Apple's rise is consistent with the spec's note that a two-sided hit could be stronger.
7. Host/guest fake sockets (sync7.mjs, no network code, relay untouched): all 7, 160 frames each, 0 differences in every fighter's place, percent, hitstun, burn, slow, stun and stocks; the move's prop (f._nsv) is identical on the guest on every frame it exists; drawing it on the guest threw 0 errors; final percents equal. Tapey's trap arrives on the guest at the same place with its tape-deck shape (same as Cherries' trap; the wire drops the trap flag for every trap). Limit: the guest here only applies snapshots, it does not run its own hits, so "hits match" means the host's hits arrive. Run at the spec numbers, before the step 8 changes.
8. Chain check (chain.mjs): Tapey: foe stuck 46 frames vs old 45 at the shipped spec (stun 40); shortened to 39: now 45, equal. The hit comes at frame 29 (the trap arms after 24), so the follow-up is later and needs the foe to walk into it. Tissues: total incl. poison 20.2 vs old 20 at the spec damage 11; lowered to 10.5: 19.2.
