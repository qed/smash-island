## Tactics found so far (the owner asked, 2026-10-28: "any tactics that you found based on the world cups?")

For a player against these CPUs (Expert), from cups #1-#13:
1. **Go deep, they won't follow** (#4, #7, #13): CPUs judge a falling foe as gone by distance and barely edge-guard, and they never lay traps at the ledge. Chasing a hit off-stage is safe if your recovery is decent. **Out of date since the bot fixes (2026-10-29): #30 had three ledgeguards for stocks in one group stage.**
2. **Bait the punish** (#9): Expert CPUs wait out a swing just out of their reach, then step in to punish it. Throw a slow, obvious move at the edge of your reach and hit them as they step in.
3. **Traps are free hits** (#3, #6, #8, #11, #13): CPUs walk straight at you and don't treat laid traps, prisms or puddles as danger. Put one between you and them; one by an item works even better (#8: they walk into hazards to grab items).
4. **Burn the counter, then go in** (#6): a CPU guesses its counter when it sees any swing start nearby -- a shot or a feint counts -- and then can't guess again for 4-7 s. Feint, let the counter whiff, then attack.
5. **Reach beats speed** (#13): a CPU starts swinging from its own reach, so a jab a little longer than theirs wins every trade. Stand just outside their reach, inside yours.
6. **Dash fighters near the edge** (#10): CPUs fire full-length dashes at you even near the ledge (Leafy's kamikaze Sprint). Stand by the ledge and step aside.
7. **Punish the empty juice** (#13): OJ is moody for 4 s after a Juice Spill pour -- he hits harder but flies further. That's the moment to hit him.
8. **No timer in knockouts** (#7, #10): fighters who win by surviving (Match) have to take three stocks in a cup final; fighters who almost never die (Bow) can outlast everyone.
9. **The low-rankers make the plays** (#8-#10): Ruby, Pillow and Bow made the play of the match while losing. Bow and Pillow especially are much better in a player's hands than as CPUs (their CPUs skip their best moves).

Several of these are CPU gaps that the queued bot fixes close (edge-guarding, hazard sense, kit misreads), so they'll work less well afterwards.
